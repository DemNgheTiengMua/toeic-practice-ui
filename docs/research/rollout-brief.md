# Research brief — Sequencing the rebuild without breaking the product

**Audience:** one external deep-research agent. **No repository access** — self-contained.

**What you deliver:** a migration and rollout plan for the whole rebuild: what changes in what order, what
the product looks like at each step, how old data is handled, and what is deleted when.

**Scope:** the sequence and the data. Not the design of any subsystem — five sibling briefs own those.

**Why this exists:** placement, the learning path, the learner interface, the admin tools and the
surviving subsystems are all being changed, and they are not independent. A band means one thing today
and another after the rebuild. If any of them ships alone, the product is internally inconsistent — and
the worst case is not a crash, it is a learner seeing two different answers to "what is my level" on the
same day.

---

## 1. The product, and the shape of the change

A multi-certificate English exam-practice application. Learners take a placement test, follow a learning
path, drill, and sit mock exams. Five certificates, and **they do not all have four skills** — two examine
only Listening and Reading, one examines only Speaking and Writing.

What is being rebuilt:

| Area | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Content** | Items gain a CEFR level; a shared pool serves all certificates; rubrics gain band descriptors |
| **Placement** | Two-stage adaptive, four skills, CEFR results per skill, a new item budget within 60 minutes |
| **Path** | Sliding four-week window, skill-weighted steps, mastery-driven step size, free drilling gated by the week |
| **Learner UI** | Every screen that shows a band, a plan, a session or a score |
| **Admin** | Authoring tools that enforce the content specification, and rubric authoring that does not exist today |
| **Survivors** | Mock exam, insight, weakness analysis, calibration, goal feasibility — all read a band and must be reconciled |

---

## 2. What makes this hard

### 2.1 The unit of meaning changes underneath everything

Today a **Band** is a row in a certificate's own band table. After the rebuild, placement produces
**CEFR** — a different quantity. Two of five certificates have **no CEFR-named band at all**.

So there is a period during which the product contains both. Everything that reads a band either has to
work in both worlds or must not ship until the world changes.

### 2.2 The content does not exist yet

The new pool must be authored, validated, reviewed and loaded before placement or path can use it. The
current bank is 116 items with zero at the hardest level and twelve of fifteen topics empty. You cannot
launch a four-skill placement against that.

### 2.3 The learner's history is load-bearing

Attempts already exist, with bands recorded under the old model. Mastery records exist, keyed to topics.
Paths exist, with steps. Insights exist, quoting numbers that will no longer mean the same thing.

Deleting them is an option — the product is not in production — but it is not the only option, and the
choice must be made deliberately rather than by a cascade.

### 2.4 There are no migrations

The schema is a single SQL file applied to a fresh database. There is no migration framework. That makes
"start clean" cheap and makes "preserve history" expensive. Say which you would choose, and for what.

### 2.5 A confirmed defect already shows how this fails

A placement containing free responses cannot be completed: the attempt stays `submitted` with no band,
and a second writer marks the placement `done` with a null band, which a constraint correctly refuses.
**Two writers disagreeing about one row.** Any rollout that has two writers for one fact during a
transition will produce the same class of failure.

---

## 3. Decisions already taken

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **This is a delete-and-rebuild, not an incremental extension.** | Patching the old placement and the old path has already been attempted; both carry design faults that a patch preserves. |
| R2 | **Old code paths are deleted once their replacement is green**, not left behind a flag forever. | Two code paths for one behaviour drift apart. |
| R3 | **Content is generated by AI, auto-validated, then a 10% sample is human-reviewed.** | Reviewing every item is not realistic. |
| R4 | **The ledger is append-only.** | Historical transaction rows must stay readable. |
| R5 | **One writer per fact.** | A confirmed defect class, not a theoretical one. |
| R6 | **The product is not in production**, so downtime is acceptable and "start clean" is available. | This is the cheapest moment this migration will ever have. |

## 4. What the plan must answer

### 4.1 The sequence

1. **What order do the six areas change in**, and what does the product look like at each step? A step
   must leave the product usable, or explicitly take it offline for a stated window.
2. **Which steps can ship behind a flag, and which cannot?** A flag is only useful if both paths can
   coexist without disagreeing.
3. **What is the first thing to ship**, and what does it prove? Name the smallest change that de-risks
   the rest.
4. **What is the last thing switched off**, and what must be true before the old path is deleted?

### 4.2 Content before code

5. **How much content must exist before placement can launch**, and before the path can launch? The
   content brief derives the volume; you decide the threshold.
6. **What does the product do while content is thin** — a partial rollout to one certificate, a
   restricted level range, or nothing until the pool is full? The owner's product has five certificates
   and the pool is shared; a partial launch is possible and may be right.
7. **How is content versioned** so that a bad batch can be withdrawn without losing learner history that
   references it?

### 4.3 Old data

8. **What happens to existing attempts** whose bands were recorded under the old model? Rendered as-is,
   converted, hidden, or deleted?
9. **What happens to existing mastery records**, keyed to the old topic taxonomy, when topics change?
10. **What happens to existing learners' paths** — regenerated, frozen, or dropped?
11. **What happens to AI insights already stored**, which quote band values that will mean something else?
12. **What is the rule that decides preserve versus drop** for each of the above? State it once rather
    than per table.

### 4.4 Coexistence and the two-writer problem

13. **During the transition, how is it guaranteed that only one writer produces a derived snapshot** —
    placement result, attempt band, path step state?
14. **What invariant can be asserted in the database** so a two-writer bug fails loudly at write time
    rather than silently at read time? The existing constraints caught one instance; say whether that
    approach generalises.
15. **What is the rollback story** for each step, given there are no migrations?

### 4.5 Verification

16. **How do you know a step is safe to ship?** The product has a full test suite and a schema harness;
    say what evidence is required per step beyond "tests pass".
17. **What canary or smoke check** proves the product works after a step, for a learner who is mid-path
    and for one who has never taken a placement?
18. **How is "the product is consistent" defined**, so it can be checked rather than assumed? For
    example: no screen shows two different levels for the same skill on the same day.

---

## 5. Deliverable format

A migration and rollout plan with:

1. Scope and assumptions.
2. **The step sequence**, each step with: what changes, what the product looks like after, what is
   flagged, how it is verified, and how it rolls back.
3. **A data disposition table** — every table that holds a band, a level or a derived value, and
   preserve/convert/drop for each, with the rule that decided it.
4. **The single-writer guarantee** — how it is enforced during and after the transition.
5. **Content readiness gates** — how much content, for which certificates, before which step.
6. **Verification plan** per step.
7. **Open questions** with options and your recommendation.
8. **References** to real sources on large-scale schema replacement, expand–contract migration, and
   feature-flag sequencing.

---

## 6. Known gaps, deliberately not solved here

- **The design of any subsystem.** Five sibling briefs own placement, path, learner UI, admin and the
  surviving readers.
- **Pricing.** Out of scope.
- **Accessibility and mobile.** Out of scope for v1.

---

## 7. What to be careful about

- **"Start clean" is available but not free.** It costs every existing learner's history. Say what you
  would preserve and why, rather than defaulting to either extreme.
- **A flag is not a plan.** Two code paths coexisting is a temporary state with a defined end, not a way
  to avoid choosing.
- **Do not sequence by what is easiest to build.** Sequence by what keeps the product consistent. The
  hardest constraint is that a learner must never see two different answers to "what is my level".
- **Content is the long pole.** Code can be written in parallel; 500 validated items cannot. If your
  sequence assumes content will be ready when the code is, say so explicitly as an assumption.
- **Two writers, again.** The one confirmed defect in this rebuild is two writers disagreeing about one
  row. Look for the others before they ship.
- **No migrations means every step is a rebuild of the database.** Say how that is done without losing
  data that the previous step created.
