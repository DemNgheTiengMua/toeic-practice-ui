# **Software Requirements Specification: Content Warehouse, Authoring Pipeline, and Staff Governance Interface**

## **1\. System Boundaries and Operational Exclusions**

### **1.1 Architectural Purpose and the Warehouse Paradigm**

This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the staff-facing administration, authoring, automated validation, human review, rubric calibration, and lifecycle governance interfaces for a multi-certificate English assessment platform. The foundational architectural premise of this specification is that the staff interface operates as a shared psychometric item warehouse rather than a single-exam form editor. Every assessment unit—comprising shared stimuli (reading passages and audio tracks), objective multiple-choice child items, productive free-response tasks, and level-tagged instructional lessons—is authored, validated, and stored against three primary canonical coordinates: skill (\\text{Listening}, \\text{Reading}, \\text{Speaking}, \\text{Writing}), cefr\_level (\\text{A1} through \\text{C2}), and topic\_id (1\\text{ of }20 canonical communicative micro-skills), supplemented by an orthogonal domain\_context (\\text{workplace}, \\text{academic}, \\text{general}) attribute.  
Five distinct certificate configurations draw dynamically or via blueprint assembly from this unified pool, and those certificates do not examine an identical number of skills. While **TOEIC Listening & Reading (4-Skill Configuration)**, **IELTS Academic & General Training**, and **VSTEP (Vietnam National Standardized Test, Levels 3–5)** evaluate all four macro-skills, **TOEIC Multi-Stage Adaptive (CAT)** evaluates exclusively Listening and Reading, and **TOEIC Speaking & Writing** evaluates exclusively Speaking and Writing. Consequently, no authoring screen, upload schema, or review workflow may bind an item directly to a single certificate or exam during creation; certificate-specific behaviors exist strictly at the blueprint query, scale projection, and presentation layers.  
The necessity for a specification-enforcing staff interface is demonstrated by an empirical audit of the legacy database. In the absence of schema-enforced level envelopes, coverage heatmaps, and mandatory rubric matrices, the legacy environment accumulated only 116 total items across five certificates and four skills, with zero items at the hardest difficulty band (\\text{C2}), 12 of 15 legacy topic categories completely empty, eight exam sections declaring 30 or more items while holding single-digit counts, five exam sections containing zero items, an IELTS mock exam declaring 40 items while holding only 2, and productive grading criteria lacking band descriptors at every level. Furthermore, three historical bypasses around exam publishing allowed staff to ship zero-question exams via a status dropdown, pass empty sections via blank expected counts, and overwrite live exam items that learners had already completed. The tooling specified herein makes these failure modes structurally impossible while reducing authoring friction.

| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Pool Ownership Model** | Exam-bound authoring; items created inside individual exam sections. | Shared warehouse pool indexed by (skill, cefr\_level, topic\_id, domain\_context). | Prevents item duplication across 5 certificates and 2-skill vs. 4-skill schema breaks. |

### **1.2 Operational Exclusions and Non-Goals**

To preserve strict separation of architectural concerns across the platform, several adjacent operational domains are explicitly excluded from the staff warehouse and authoring surface. Learner-facing delivery interfaces—including test-player screen layouts, countdown timer animations, client-side audio player scrubbing controls, and web or mobile state management—are governed by a separate learner interface specification, though the staff surface provides a sandboxed preview modal that renders items inside those client skins. Runtime psychometric routing engines, including Item Response Theory (IRT) ability estimation (\\hat{\\theta}\_s), Maximum Information item selection during the 60-minute two-stage placement sitting, Bayesian posterior updating, and spaced-repetition temporal decay scheduling for daily review queues, operate as headless backend consumers of the active warehouse pool rather than interactive staff screens. Commercial monetization systems—spanning payment gateways, subscription tier entitlements, packages, orders, and user wallets—are likewise excluded. Existing operational views for mentor management and general user administration remain outside the content rebuild, intersecting with this specification solely where learner complaints trigger a formal content-defect escalation workflow. Finally, by recorded product ownership decision, the v1 staff interface targets a desktop-first viewport of 1440 \\times 900\\text{ px} and excludes mobile responsive layouts and formal accessibility certification.

## **2\. Permission Architecture and Role Model**

Treating item writing, psychometric review, certificate blueprint architecture, and learner complaint triage as a single monolithic administrator role caused review to become informal and turned the publish audit gate into an adversarial obstacle. In a high-integrity digital item bank, access control must be defined through granular, composable permission sets rather than rigid job titles, enforcing separation of duties at both the API controller and database transaction layers.

### **2.1 Granular Permission Sets and Separation of Duties**

The staff governance model partitions system capabilities into five orthogonal permission namespaces: pool.content.author, pool.content.review, assessment.blueprint.design, operations.support.triage, and system.governance.admin. While a smaller operational team may assign multiple permission namespaces to a single staff account, the system enforces two immutable separation-of-duty invariants at the database transaction layer that no role combination can override. First, under the **No Self-Approval Invariant (reviewer\_id \!= author\_id)**, an account holding both pool.content.author and pool.content.review is programmatically blocked from claiming, adjudicating, or clearing any 10% stratified human review sample for a batch or individual item where that same user\_id is recorded as uploaded\_by or last\_modified\_by. Second, under the **Read-Only Triage Invariant**, accounts resolving learner complaints under operations.support.triage may inspect full item schemas, historical versions, evidence\_span alignments, and learner attempt payloads, and may lodge an *Item Defect Escalation* into the review queue, but possess zero write privileges on item text, answer keys, rubrics, or exam structures.

### **4.2 Deep Specification of Screen S-05 & S-06: Single-Item and Shared-Stimulus Authoring (Answering Q5, Q8, Q9, Q10, Q11)**

The legacy exam studio was reduced from three panes to two and from 18 visible controls per multiple-choice question to 14, yet authoring 200 questions remained painful enough that authors abandoned exams mid-build. Analysis of the legacy 14-control form reveals the root cause of that cognitive load: it forced the author to re-specify exam-section bindings, stimulus-level metadata, scoring weights, and redundant formatting fields on every single question, while failing to visually link shared passages or audio tracks to their child items.  
In the rebuilt **Master-Detail Stimulus & Child Item Studio (S-05)**, parent stimulus metadata is authored once in the Left Pane (640px width at 1440 \\times 900), and child items are authored in a vertical accordion stack in the Right Pane (800px width). By inheriting skill, domain\_context, content\_tier, and default question\_type and time\_limit\_sec from the parent stimulus format and CEFR envelope rules, **each child 4-MCQ item requires exactly 9 interactive controls**—a 35.7\\% reduction below the 14-control ceiling and a 50.0\\% reduction from the original 18 controls.

#### **Authoring the Two Hard Shapes: Shared Passages and Shared Audio Tracks (Answering Q9)**

Standard web forms fail on one-to-many stimulus bundles because they either force authors to navigate back and forth between separate parent and child screens or duplicate the passage across multiple tabs. #### **The Audio Attachment, Forced Alignment, and Trimming Dock (S-06 — Answering Q10)**

Replacing a raw file path text box and hand-typed millisecond numbers with a file picker and two player buttons (\[ Set Start \] and \[ Set End \]) was the right directional move toward direct manipulation, but it remains an incomplete model for multi-certificate listening assessment. A simple file picker and two live-playback buttons leave four critical operational gaps unresolved. First, clicking a button while audio is playing introduces 200\\text{--}400\\text{ms} of human reaction-time jitter, frequently slicing off word-initial plosives or trailing sibilants. Second, in a shared dialogue or monologue serving 2 to 4 child items, the author must define not only the master clip boundaries, but also the individual evidence\_span\[span\_103\](start\_span)\[span\_103\](end\_span).start\_sec and end\_sec timestamps for each child item—and must verify that adjacent detail or inference items are separated by at least 8.0\\text{ seconds} of audio playback. Third, a basic audio player cannot verify whether a claimed A2 track actually speaks at 115\\text{--}130\\text{ wpm} within 30\\text{--}45\\text{s} or whether an A1 track maintains \\ge 0.8\\text{s} inter-clause pauses. Fourth, if the uploaded audio diverges by even a single word from the written text\_or\_transcript, learners reading post-test explanations receive corrupted text.  
To complete the model, the **Acoustic Waveform & Whisper Alignment Dock (S-06)** retains the intuitive file picker and \[ Set Start \] / \[ Set End \] buttons while embedding four automated acoustic engineering layers. Authors may either upload an external studio .wav/.mp3 file or synthesize multi-speaker dialogue directly from the written script by assigning each speaker a canonical accent (en-US, en-GB, en-AU, en-CA, en-IE, tracking the 45\\% / 35\\% / 20\\% international accent balance) and neural voice profile. Immediately upon file upload or synthesis, the backend executes Whisper-Large-v3 forced alignment in under 3\\text{ seconds}, mapping every word token in text\_or\_tran\[span\_105\](start\_span)\[span\_105\](end\_span)script to its exact \[start\_ms, end\_ms\] coordinates on a visual SVG waveform. Any word where ASR transcription diverges from the written script highlights in crimson (ASR WER \> 0.0%), blocking Gate 2 clearance until the author corrects the transcript or regenerates the audio. When the author clicks \[ Set Start \] or \[ Set End \] (or drags the master trim handles), the marker automatically snaps to the nearest zero-crossing silence window with a 50\\text{\[span\_256\](start\_span)\[span\_256\](end\_span)ms} acoustic buffer, eliminating clipped phonemes. Finally, to bind evidence\_span.start\_sec and end\_sec for Child Item k, the author simply highlights the target sentence in the time-aligned transcript; the dock automatically binds the exact timestamps, enforces chronological ordering across child items (Item 1 \< Item 2 \< Item 3, except L-GIST at sequence\_index \= 1), displays a live Inter-Item Acoustic Gap Badge (Gap: 11.4s \[OK\] vs. Gap: 5.2s \[Below 8.0s Minimum\]), and scans all child stems and options via regex to prohibit any reference to player timestamps.

#### **The Defensible CEFR Level Control (Answering Q11)**

A plain six-value dropdown (A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2) is effortless to render and guaranteed to produce miscalibrated items because authors select the level they *intend* to write rather than what the stimulus and task cognitively demand. In S-05 and S-07, the CEFR Level Control is engineered as a Dual-Constraint Psychometric Selector. On the parent stimulus (L\_s), selecting a band in the 6-level segmented control expands a persistent **4-Gauge Live Envelope Strip** directly beneath the selector that evaluates the stimulus in real time against the deterministic bounds in Section 2 of the content specification. Gauge 1 validates word count or audio duration (such as 220\\text{--}300\\text{ words} for B1 Reading or 45\\text{--}75\\text{s} for B1 Listening); Gauge 2 computes lexical profile coverage against EVP / Oxford 5000 and Academic Word List (AWL) bounds (such as \\ge 90\\% A1–B1 families and \\le 3\\% B2 tokens for B1, underlining out-of-band words in purple inside the text editor); Gauge 3 computes Mean Length of T-Unit (\\text{MLTU}), clauses per T-unit, and Flesch-Kincaid Grade on every sentence edit (such as \\text{MLTU } 10.5\\text{--}13.5\\text{ words}, 1.3\\text{--}1.6\\text{ clauses/T-unit}, and FK Grade 6.0\\text{--}8.0 for B1); and Gauge 4 verifies acoustic speech rate (\\text{wpm}) and pause floors for Listening. If any gauge falls outside the selected band's envelope, the control displays a prominent **Empirical Band Mismatch** warning showing which band the text's measured metrics actually fit.  
On each child item (L\_i), the CEFR level selector is structurally locked by the **\\pm 1 Band Envelope Rule**: when L\_s \\in \\{\\text{A1}, \\text{A2}\\}, only L\_i \= L\_s is enabled; when L\_\[span\_260\](start\_span)\[span\_260\](end\_span)s \\in \\{\\text{B1..C2}\\}, bands outside \\{L\_s-1, L\_s, L\_s+1\\} are disabled (disabled="disabled"), and the bundle header enforces that at least two-thirds (\\ge 66\\%) of child items match L\_s exactly. During Gate 4 validation, an independent classifier predicts \\hat{L}\_i from the stimulus, stem, and options, blocking promotion if \\hat{L}\_i \\neq L\_i.

### **5.1 Workflow 1: Bulk JSON Upload, Partial Commit, and 10% Stratified SME Review (Answering Q2, Q3, Q4)**

Decisions A1 and A2 establish that content is generated by AI, validated automatically across four gates, uploaded as one JSON file per skill (listening\_pool.json, reading\_pool.json, speaking\_pool.json, writing\_pool.json), and human-reviewed via a 10% stratified sample without a pilot study. A primary engineering requirement is that **a file of 300 items where item 247 is wrong must never lose or block the 246 valid items before it**.  
When an author holding pool.content.author drops a skill file—for example, reading\_pool.json containing 100 shared stimulus groups totaling 300 child items tagged with batch\_2026\_11\_r\_b2—into the **Bulk JSON Ingestion & Staging Workbench (S-02)**, the ingestion engine validates the top-level envelope (schema\_version \== "2.0.0", skill \== "reading") and decomposes the payload into independent **Stimulus-Group Units of Work** inside the pool\_staging\_units table rather than wrapping all 300 items in a monolithic database transaction. Each unit of work streams through the four automated validation gates enforcing strict cross-model family separation (\\text{Autho\[span\_150\](start\_span)\[span\_150\](end\_span)r\\\_LLM} \\neq \\text{Verifier\\\_LLM\\\_1 \\& 2} \\neq \\text{Grader\\\_LLM}). Gate 1 checks structural and Haladyna hygiene (4 options, zero meta-options such as all/none of the above, key word count within \\pm 25\\% of the distractor mean, part-of-speech parallelism, and option key balance of 20\\text{--}30\\% per letter A–D). Gate 2 verifies linguistic and acoustic envelopes (word count, FK Grade, \\text{MLTU}\[span\_151\](start\_span)\[span\_151\](end\_span), clauses per T-unit, lexical coverage \\ge 85\\text{--}95\\%, Whisper-Large-v3 ASR \\text{WER} \= 0.0\\%, speech rate in \\text{wpm}, and inter-item audio gaps \\ge 8.0\\text{s}). Gate 3 executes blind dual-model key solving where Verifier\_LLM\_1 and Verifier\_LLM\_2 independently solve each objective item without seeing correct\_option (both must choose the key with P \\ge 0.85) followed by a stimulus-blind ablation check (rejecting the item if either solver selects the key with P \\ge 0.50 when the passage or transcript is withheld). Gate 4 verifies level consistency (\\hat{L}\_i \= L\_i), sibling reading evidence span independence (\\text{IoU} \< 0.15), and the 6-response synthetic grading ladder for productive tasks.  
Suppose Item 247 (which is child item 2 of 3 inside Stimulus Group 82, located at JSON line 4,812) fails Gate 3 because Verifier\_LLM\_2 selects Option B (P \= 0.62) over key Option D, or fails schema validation due to a missing distr\[span\_310\](start\_span)\[span\_310\](end\_span)actor\_rationale. Because validation commits at the stimulus\_group unit-of-work boundary, the **99 valid stimulus groups (297 items: items 1–245 and 248–300) immediately enter staging\_\[span\_311\](start\_span)\[span\_311\](end\_span)status \= 'validated\_ready'**, while only Stimulus Group 82 (containing Item 247 and its two sibling items 246 and 248\) is isolated into staging\_status \= 'staging\_rejected'. The author can either click **\[Commit Validated Subset Now (297 Items)\]**—which immediately promotes the 297 valid items to the 10% SME review stage while routing Stimulus Group 82 to the **Author Remediation Workbench (S-04)**—or click **\[Fix Item 247 Inline\]** to open Stimulus Group 82 inside an embedded S-05 studio drawer, rewrite the ambiguous distractor on Item 247, re-run Gates 1–4 on that single bundle in under 4\\text{ seconds}, and commit all 300 items without ever re-uploading the JSON file. The fate of the **90% unsampled items** is governed deterministically by the reviewer's adjudication of that 10% sample. If the 10% sample exhibits **zero answer-key errors (0\\%) and \\le 5\\% non-key defects**, submitting the audit executes an atomic database transaction that marks the 10% sample human\_reviewed \= true and **simultaneously promotes 100% of the stratum (both the 10% reviewed sample and the 90% unsampled buffer, minus any individual non-key rejected item) to lifecycle\_s\[span\_158\](start\_span)\[span\_158\](end\_span)tatus \= 'active'**. Conversely, under the **Zero-Tolerance Key Rule**, if the human reviewer discovers even **a single answer-key error** in the 10% sample, the system immediately aborts promotion of the 90% buffer and flips **100% of that (skill, cefr\_level) stratum to lifecycle\_status \= 'quarantined'**, requiring full human key verification or regeneration of the entire stratum before any item in it can become active. Similarly, if \> 5\\% of sampled items fail non-key human criteria (such as CEFR level drift or robotic TTS prosody), the entire stratum (100%) is rejected back to S-04 (lifecycle\_status \= 'draft').

### **5.4 Workflow 4: Structurally Locked Exam Resolution via Edition Forking (Answering Q16)**

Under Decision A4, the instant \\ge 1 learner completes an attempt on a published exam, the database sets structural\_lock \= true on that exam record (Edition v1), permanently prohibiting adding, removing, or reordering items, editing answer keys, altering question types, or changing section timers in place. ### **5.5 Workflow 5: Live Item Quarantine, Copy-on-Write Versioning, and Retirement (Answering Q7)**

Every active item in the warehouse is continuously monitored by the psychometric telemetry daemon at N \= 30, 50, 100, and 250 learner administrations in **Screen S-10**, and can also be flagged via an *Item Defect Escalation* submitted by operations.support.triage from a learner complaint. When an author or reviewer opens a quarantined or active item in S-05 to repair its text or answer key, **in-place mutation of the existing version (version \= 1\) is blocked by the database if \\ge 1 learner attempt references (item\_id, version \= 1\)**. In an assessment warehouse serving active learners across five certificates, state-mutating or destructive operations—such as deleting an exam section, retiring an item or stimulus bundle, or deactivating a certificate—carry severe relational and psychometric blast radii. Furthermore, under Generalizability Theory, score variance across persons (p) and tasks (t) decomposes into \\sigma^2(X\_{pt}) \= \\sigma^2\_p \+ \\sigma^2\_t \+ \\sigma^2\_{pt} \+ \\sigma^2\_e (with empirical proportions averaging \\sigma^2\_p \= 0.55, \\sigma^2\_t \= 0.03, \\sigma^2\_{pt} \= 0.27, and \\sigma^2\_e \= 0.15), yielding an absolute dependability index of \\Phi(n\_t) \= \\frac{0.55}{0.55 \+ \\frac{0.45}{n\_t}}. Because a single productive task yields \\Phi(1) \= 0.550 (\\text{SEM} \= \\pm 0.67\\text{ bands}, suitable only for formative feedback), diagnostic placement requires n\_t \= 2\\text{ tasks} (\\Phi(2) \= 0.710, E\\rho^2 \= 0.724, \\text{SEM} \= \\pm 0.47\\text{ bands}), peripheral topic mastery (A1, C2) requires n\_t \= 3\\text{ tasks} (\\Phi(3) \= 0.786, E\\rho^2 \= 0.797, \\text{SEM} \= \\pm 0.39\\text{ bands}), and core skill promotion (A2–C1) requires n\_t \= 4\\tex\[span\_185\](start\_span)\[span\_185\](end\_span)t{ tasks} (\\Phi(4) \= 0.830, E\\rho^2 \= 0.840, \\text{SEM} \= \\pm 0.34\\text{ bands}). Consequently, retiring even a single productive task or objective stimulus bundle without replacement can drop a topic cell below the minimum dependability floor required to issue high-stakes mastery decisions, while allowing a drifting item (r\_{\\text{pbis}} \< 0.10 or p \\notin \[0.30, 0.92\] at N \= 50\) to remain active corrupts learner placement.

### **7.1 Soft versus Hard Deletion Policy by Content Entity (Answering Q18)**

## **8\. Open Architectural Questions and Recommendations**

While core psychometric parameters and lifecycle states are fixed by Decisions A1–A6 and the content specification, eight architectural and operational questions across the staff interface and warehouse configuration require explicit evaluation of options and formal recommendations.

### **8.1 Resolution of Staff Interface and Warehouse Architecture Questions**

First, when a warehouse item pinned inside a **Structurally Locked Fixed Mock Exam (Edition v1)** is marked retired\_defective, the system must reconcile Decision A4 (locked exams cannot mutate their item structure in place) with the imperative to prevent new mock test-takers from encountering a known broken question. Leaving Edition v1 live until an architect manually forks Edition v2 (Option A) exposes learners to the defective item in the interim, whereas silently swapping the item inside Edition v1 (Option B) violates Decision A4 by changing the item composition recorded under Edition v1. The recommended architecture (**Option C: Automated Copy-on-Write Edition Fork with Isomorph Swap**) triggers an automatic background fork to Edition v2 whenever a pinned item enters retire\[span\_205\](start\_span)\[span\_205\](end\_span)d\_defective, substitutes the highest-discrimination active item (isomorph) from the identical (skill, topic\_id, cefr\_level, question\_type, domain\_context) cell, verifies all six publish invariants (INV-1..6), and promotes Edition v2 while archiving Edition v1.  
Second, in **Screen S-03 (10% Stratified SME Audit Queue)**, revealing the AI-generated correct\[span\_207\](start\_span)\[span\_207\](end\_span)\_option and explanation immediately upon loading an item (Option A) invites anchoring bias, tempting human reviewers to rubber-stamp plausible AI keys. Because the 10% human sample governs promotion of the 90% unsampled stratum buffer, the recommended design (**Option B: Mandatory Blind-First Click Before Diagnostic Reveal**) masks correct\_option and requires the human SME to click their own answer choice (A–D) before unlocking the diagnostic reveal panel and approval controls.  
Third, to prevent lost work when multiple authors repair items in **Screen S-04 / S-05**, relying on last-write-wins HTTP POST overwrites (Option A) risks one author clobbering another's audio trim or child item edits, whereas full real-time CRDT co-editing (Option C) introduces unnecessary infrastructure complexity. The recommended design (**Option B: Optimistic Concurrency via etag \+ 15-Minute Heartbeat Lease**) acquires a soft 15-minute lease (locked\_by\_user\_id) when a stimulus group is opened in S-05, displays a live presence badge to other staff, and validates the version etag on save.  
Fourth, for **Mock Exam Section Assembly in S-09**, restricting mock exams solely to fixed item lists (Option A) causes learners who have practiced extensively in the warehouse to encounter previously seen items during mock exams, whereas building a separate locked mock reserve of 2 fixed forms per certificate (Option C) requires authoring \+416\\text{ additional units} (2,536\\text{ total}). The recommended design (**Option B: Dual-Mode Blueprint Supporting Dynamic Unseen Assembly \+ Pinned Cohort Forms**) defaults mock sections to dynamic stratified queries filtered by WHERE item\_id NOT IN (:seen\_item\_ids) (requiring 0 extra items beyond the 2,120 pool) while allowing institutional administrators to pin fixed benchmark editions when cohort comparability is required.
