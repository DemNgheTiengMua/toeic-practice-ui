# **Software Requirements Specification: Adaptive Placement Test Rebuild for Multi-Certificate English Assessment Platform**

## **Section 1: Operational Boundaries, System Context, and Architectural Mandates**

### **1.1 Operational Mandate and System Context**

This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the complete engineering, psychometric, and behavioral architecture for rebuilding the placement test subsystem of a multi-certificate English exam-practice application. The rebuild executes a clean-slate replacement (delete-and-rebuild) of the legacy placement implementation, which suffered from five compounding structural defects that rendered its diagnostic output untrustworthy. Specifically, the legacy 24-question TOEIC diagnostic contained zero items at difficulty levels 4 and 5 (distributing 4, 11, and 9 items across levels 1, 2, and 3), making it mathematically incapable of separating B2 proficiency from C1 proficiency despite reporting a starting band that governed downstream curriculum generation and study-time feasibility verdicts. Furthermore, the legacy diagnostics measured only Listening and Reading while omitting Speaking and Writing entirely, relied on deterministic selection that served byte-identical papers on every retake, attached arbitrary DifficultyLevel 1–5 integers with no mapping to the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) or any certificate scale, and suffered from a fatal dual-writer database constraint failure whenever free-response tasks entered asynchronous AI grading.  
The platform supports five distinct certificate configurations that vary in both the language modalities they examine and the score scales they report to candidates. In the product workflow, a learner selects a target certificate, establishes a study objective (starting band, target band, target exam date, and weekly study hours), completes a placement test to establish baseline proficiency, and follows an automated learning path. While the internal placement engine, item pool, and scoring model operate on a unified, certificate-agnostic CEFR axis, the active certificate selected by the learner governs which skills are administered during a placement sitting and how canonical CEFR coordinates are projected onto native certificate bands at the reporting layer.

| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |

### **1.2 Core Architectural Invariants and Settled Decisions**

Four immutable architectural invariants govern every component of the placement subsystem. First, placement is permanently free of charge; initiating, resuming, or retaking a placement sitting must never generate a credit ledger row or invoke billing deduction logic. Second, strict cross-certificate isolation is enforced at the reporting boundary so that one certificate's native band definitions or scale conversions never leak into another certificate's evaluation. Third, algorithmic authority is strictly separated from generative artificial intelligence: automated evaluators (LLMs and acoustic prosody models) are restricted exclusively to scoring productive free responses against explicit analytical rubrics and authoring diagnostic narrative commentary, whereas item selection, stage routing, score aggregation, and band resolution are executed by deterministic code. Fourth, every band classification and scale crosswalk is resolved from versioned database lookup tables rather than hardcoded application branches or invented ad-hoc mappings.  
Within these invariants, the system architecture operationalizes all 23 settled decisions (D1–D23) and the core storage mandate (M1) established in the architectural brief. Under Decisions D1, D12, and D13, placement returns one discrete CEFR band (Pre-A1, A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2) paired with an intra-band position integer (0–100) for each examined skill, alongside a single **Overall CEFR band defined strictly as the weakest measured skill** rather than an average. Averaging across modalities conceals asymmetric profiles—such as a candidate who reads at B2 but speaks at A2—and misleads the learner into believing their overall readiness is B1. Presenting the discrete band and the 0–100 intra-band position together as an inseparable pair (D12) allows learners to track fine-grained progress across retakes (e.g., advancing from B2 · 12 to B2 · 78\) while preventing confusion when crossing a band boundary resets the intra-band position toward the bottom of the new, higher band (B2 · 88 advancing to C1 · 14).  
Under Decisions D2, D3, D4, D16, and D18, DifficultyLevel 1–5 is eliminated and replaced by attaching a canonical cefr\_level (A1–C2) directly to every item and task inside a single unified item pool shared across all five certificates. Internal scoring, adaptive routing, and intra-band position calculations execute exclusively on the CEFR axis and never on a certificate's native score scale (D16), preventing the structural breakdown that would otherwise occur on certificates whose internal band codes are not CEFR-named (CAT\_B1, CAT\_B2, SW\_B1, SW\_B2). Each placement sitting measures only the skills examined by the learner's active certificate (D4), while preserving any valid skill result measured within the preceding **90 calendar days** (D18) so that switching from a two-skill certificate (TOEIC\_CAT) to a four-skill certificate (IELTS) reuses fresh Listening and Reading coordinates without forcing redundant testing.  
For the objective skills (Listening and Reading), Decisions D9, D10, D11, D14, and D15 mandate a two-stage multistage adaptive testing (MST) design spanning the full A1–C2 continuum with pool density concentrated at A2–C1. Linear testing was rejected because covering six bands linearly requires an excessively long paper while leaving each band sparsely sampled; item-level computerized adaptive testing (CAT) was rejected because it requires pre-calibrated item response theory (IRT) parameters (a\_i, b\_i, c\_i) from a large pilot sample that this product does not have (D9, D21). Stage 1 administers a fixed stratified routing module across bands, and Stage 2 delivers a targeted boundary module containing **at least 5 items per evaluated band** (D10) so that under a 25% 4-option multiple-choice guessing floor, a single lucky guess cannot swing a band score by 25 percentage points. A candidate is awarded objective band L only by achieving **\\ge 70\\% correct at band L and at every evaluated lower band** (D14), a rule published transparently on the results screen. When a candidate exhibits a non-monotonic response profile—such as passing B2 items while failing A2 items due to random guessing or a flawed item key—Decision D15 truncates the awarded level at the highest band of the first contiguous passing run from the bottom and logs an administrative anomaly record.  
For the productive skills (Speaking and Writing), Decisions D5, D6, D7, D17, and D19 govern task administration and asynchronous fault tolerance. The sitting enforces **no overall countdown timer**, imposing only a standardized **60-second recording limit per Speaking task** (D19) so that placement measures communicative competence rather than test-taking speed. Each productive skill administers **2 tasks** (D6) routed to match the proficiency level estimated from the objective half, balancing measurement dependability against candidate fatigue. Upon sitting submission, objective skill scores are calculated and rendered immediately while Speaking and Writing display a grading state that populates progressively as asynchronous AI evaluation completes (D5). If one productive task in a skill fails AI grading, the skill band is estimated from the remaining graded task and marked with a reduced-confidence indicator (D7); if both tasks fail grading, the skill is marked not\_measured and omitted from the overall minimum calculation so that the placement sitting still transitions to done rather than blocking completion (D17). Finally, retakes are permitted without a lifetime cap subject to a **24-hour cooldown** (D8), content is uploaded via per-skill JSON files (D20) validated automatically and audited via a **10% stratified human sample** (D21), the demo set executes end-to-end with the real AI pipeline (D22) isolated via data flags rather than separate code paths (D23), and storage reuses ExamAttempts while deriving PlacementTests via a single writer (M1).

### **1.3 Excluded Operational Domains**

Four adjacent product areas lie outside the boundaries of this specification. First, commercial packaging, paywall enforcement, and credit ledger accounting are excluded because placement sittings are unconditionally free. Second, the internal spaced-repetition and daily scheduling heuristics of the learning-path generator belong to a sibling specification; this document defines only the exact data contract that the learning-path engine consumes. Third, graphical administrative user interfaces for authoring or editing individual items are deferred (D20) in favor of bulk JSON CLI/API ingestion with line-level schema validation. Fourth, sensory and neurodivergent accessibility accommodations—such as visual sign-language substitutes for deaf candidates in Listening or orthographic error exemptions for dyslexic candidates in Writing—are excluded from v1 by owner directive and documented in Section 7.5 as future architectural hooks.

## **Section 2: Domain Model, Entity Architecture, and the CEFR Level Model**

### **2.1 Core Entities and Single-Writer Persistence Architecture**

A critical engineering driver of this rebuild is resolving the confirmed production-blocking defect documented in Section 2.5 of the architectural brief: in the legacy codebase, submitting a placement exam containing free-response tasks caused the free responses to enter pending\_ai while the attempt remained submitted with a NULL band, yet a second code path simultaneously attempted to write the PlacementTests snapshot as done with a NULL band, triggering a database constraint violation caused by two writers disagreeing about a single logical state. Under Storage Mandate M1, placement sittings continue to use ExamAttempts as the sole authoritative transactional state machine—inheriting draft synchronization, expiry sweeps, preflight checks, and navigation locks—while PlacementTests and its child skill records are maintained as a **read-optimized projection written exclusively by a single idempotent service (PlacementSnapshotProjector)**.  
In this three-tier relational architecture, client controllers and asynchronous AI grading workers mutate only the sitting execution tables (ExamAttempts, AttemptStageBundles, AttemptItemResponses, and FreeResponses). Every state transition on those tables triggers PlacementSnapshotProjector, which acquires a row-level lock on the parent ExamAttempts record, evaluates the deterministic scoring function (§4.1 of the brief), and atomically upserts PlacementTests and PlacementSkillResults. Furthermore, to satisfy Decision D17—where a sitting completes even if a productive skill fails AI grading, or where all skills fail grading during an upstream AI outage—the legacy database constraint status \= 'done' \=\> overall\_cefr IS NOT NULL on PlacementTests is replaced by the invariant check status \!= 'done' OR overall\_cefr IS NOT NULL OR completion\_failure\_reason IS NOT NULL.

### **2.2 Operational Definition of a CEFR Level and Modality Envelope**

Under Decision D2, every assessment unit carries an explicit cefr\_level (A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2). Grounded in the socio-cognitive framework of language assessment (Weir, 2005; Khalifa & Weir, 2009; Council of Europe, 2020), assigning a CEFR level to an item is not an arbitrary editorial label; it is a falsifiable psychometric claim that the item simultaneously satisfies quantitative bounds across its stimulus input complexity, its required cognitive processing operation, and its distractor or rubric demand. Moreover, because spoken discourse is transient, linear, and paced by the speaker whereas written text remains static for recursive parsing (Field, 2008; Skehan, 1998), Listening stimuli at any given CEFR band enforce a lower syntactic subordination index (\\approx 15\\text{–}20\\% shorter Mean Length of T-Unit \[MLTU\]) and higher lexical familiarity than Reading passages at the same band, while introducing controlled acoustic delivery rates and phonological reductions.

| CEFR Band | Lexical & Morphosyntactic Stimulus Envelope | Modality-Specific Input Bounds (Listening vs. Reading) | Required Cognitive Operation (Khalifa & Weir, 2009; Field, 2008\) | Productive Output & Distractor Plausibility Rule |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **A1** | \\ge 95\\% A1 tokens (top 800 word families); 0\\% idioms; MLTU 5.0–7.5 words; simple coordination (*and, but, or*). | **L:** 100–120 wpm, pauses \\ge 0.8\\text{s}, 40–65w (20–35s). **R:** 60–100w, FK Grade 1.5–3.0. | **Lexical Recognition & Verbatim Match:** Locate isolated numbers, times, or explicit words in a single clause. | **MCQ:** Distractors in same category but unmentioned/negated. **S/W:** 15–30s oral / 30–50w written basic SVO clauses. |
| **A2** | \\ge 92\\% A1–A2 tokens (top 1,800 families); MLTU 7.5–10.5 words; \\le 1.3 clauses/T-unit (*because, when, if* Type 0/1). | **L:** 115–130 wpm, minimal elision, 60–95w (30–45s). **R:** 120–180w, FK Grade 3.5–5.5. | **Local Paraphrase & Routine Transaction:** Map direct word-level synonyms across a single sentence in routine contexts. | **MCQ:** 1 verbatim keyword trap paired with wrong predicate; 2 routine options. **S/W:** 30–45s oral / 60–90w message. |
| **B1** | \\ge 90\\% A1–B1 tokens (top 3,000 families); MLTU 10.5–13.5 words; 1.3–1.6 clauses/T-unit; relatives, passives, modals. | **L:** 135–150 wpm, connected speech, 110–185w (45–75s). **R:** 220–300w, FK Grade 6.0–8.0. | **Cross-Sentence Synthesis:** Combine propositions across 2–3 adjacent sentences to identify main idea or cause-effect. | **MCQ:** All 3 distractors reuse stimulus roots; \\ge 2 test sequence reversal or misattribution. **S/W:** 45–60s / 110–150w text. |
| **B2** | \\ge 88\\% A1–B2 tokens (top 5,000 \+ AWL 1–5); MLTU 13.5–17.0 words; 1.6–2.0 clauses/T-unit; non-defining relatives, participles. | **L:** 150–165 wpm, mild reduction, 150–240w (60–90s). **R:** 320–450w, FK Grade 8.5–11.0. | **Bridging Inference & Stance Detection:** Infer unstated premises across paragraphs; detect hedging and speaker attitude. | **MCQ:** Partial truths (too broad/narrow), causal inversions, unsupported assumptions. **S/W:** 60–90s / 180–250w essay. |

| **C2** | Unrestricted lexical range; figurative and low-frequency academic lexis; MLTU \>20.0 words; embedded nominalization, inversion. | **L:** 175–195 wpm, unscripted debate, ellipsis, 260–360w (90–120s). **R:** 500–650w, FK Grade \>14.0. | **Abstract Critical Synthesis & Micro-Nuance:** Reconstruct competing implicit frameworks and stylistic irony. | **MCQ:** Near-miss analytical interpretations failing on a secondary logical entailment. **S/W:** 60s (Placement) / 280–350w critique. |

When a shared parent stimulus (stimulus\_level L\_s) supports multiple child comprehension items (item\_level L\_i), all placement routing and scoring calculations operate strictly on the child item's item\_level L\_i. To prevent construct mismatch where an advanced question is attached to a simplistic text, the content ingestion gate enforces the **\\pm 1 Band Envelope Rule**: when L\_s \\in \\{\\text{A1}, \\text{A2}\\}, every attached child item must carry L\_i \= L\_s; when L\_s \\in \\{\\text{B1}, \\text{B2}, \\text{C1}, \\text{C2}\\}, each child item must satisfy L\_i \\in \\{L\_s \- 1, L\_s, L\_s \+ 1\\}, and at least 66\\% (\\ge 2/3) of the child items attached to that stimulus must match L\_s exactly.

Orthogonal to cefr\_level, every item and task in the unified pool carries one of **20 canonical cognitive-communicative topic tags** (L-GIST, L-DET, L-INF, L-ATT, L-FUNC, L-ORG in Listening; R-GIST, R-DET, R-INF, R-ATT, R-COH, R-GRAM in Reading; S-DESC, S-INT, S-ARG, S-SYN in Speaking; and W-CORR, W-DATA, W-ARG, W-DISC in Writing). ## **Section 3: Numbered Functional Requirements**

The functional requirements below govern the complete lifecycle of a placement sitting, extending the reused mock-exam sitting engine (§4.1 of the brief) with all eight placement-specific behavioral capabilities (§4.2 of the brief).

### **3.1 Starting a Sitting, Skill Reuse, and Preflight Verification**

#### **FR-START-01: Zero-Cost Entitlement and 24-Hour Cooldown Enforcement**

When a learner requests a new placement sitting for their active certificate\_code, the sitting controller shall bypass all credit-ledger checks and never write a billing or credit consumption record. The engine shall query PlacementTests for the learner's most recent placement record; if now() \< cooldown\_until—where cooldown\_until is set to 24 hours after the completion or explicit abandonment timestamp of the prior attempt (D8)—the system shall reject attempt creation and return the exact remaining cooldown duration to the client. The sole exception to the 24-hour cooldown occurs when a prior attempt terminated with completion\_failure\_reason \= 'all\_examined\_skills\_unmeasured' due to an internal AI grading outage (D17), in which case cooldown\_until is set to now() so the learner may retry immediately.

#### **FR-START-02: Resume After Browser Tab Exit and Authoritative State Resolution (§4.2.1)**

Before creating a new ExamAttempts row, the engine shall query for any existing placement attempt owned by the learner with status \= 'in\_progress'. Because placement enforces no overall countdown timer (D19), "still in progress" is defined strictly by the server-side ExamAttempts state machine (status \= 'in\_progress' AND now() \< inactivity\_expires\_at, where inactivity\_expires\_at \= LEAST(last\_activity\_at \+ INTERVAL '48 hours', started\_at \+ INTERVAL '72 hours')), with zero elapsed time deducted while the browser tab was closed. The server-side database record (ExamAttempts, AttemptStageBundles, AttemptItemResponses, and FreeResponses) is the authoritative state for stage locks and completed tasks, while any higher-sequence unsynced draft selections buffered in client IndexedDB (client\_seq\_no \> server\_seq\_no) are merged on resume. When a learner who exited the tab returns to the application, the UI shall present an **Active Placement Resume Modal** displaying the attempt start date, completed skills, the currently active skill and stage (Part 1 or Part 2), and the count of synced responses (X of Y answered), allowing the learner either to resume at the exact item or task where they left off or to explicitly abandon the sitting.

#### **FR-START-03: Certificate Skill Filtering and 90-Day Per-Skill Result Reuse (D4, D18)**

Upon initializing a sitting, the engine shall resolve the active certificate's required skill list from the certificate configuration table (\[Listening, Reading, Speaking, Writing\] for TOEIC\_LR, IELTS, and VSTEP; \[Listening, Reading\] for TOEIC\_CAT; \[Speaking, Writing\] for TOEIC\_SW). Skills not examined by the active certificate (D4) shall never be scheduled into the sitting; any previously recorded measurements for those unexamined skills remain persisted in PlacementSkillResults (is\_examined\_by\_certificate \= false) for future certificate switches but are hidden from the active certificate's result display and excluded from overall\_cefr. For each skill examined by the active certificate, the engine shall query the learner's historical PlacementSkillResults for the newest row with skill\_status IN ('measured', 'reused\_from\_prior') whose original measurement timestamp satisfies now() \- measured\_at \<= INTERVAL '90 days' (D18). When one or more valid reusable skill results exist, the pre-sitting screen shall display each reusable skill's cefr\_level, band\_position, and exact measured\_at date, offering the learner the choice to **Reuse Valid Skills**—which copies those skill records into the new placement snapshot with skill\_status \= 'reused\_from\_prior' while preserving their original measured\_at date and administers only the missing or expired skills—or to **Re-measure All Skills**.

#### **FR-START-04: Account Tier Binding and Hardware Preflight Verification (D22, D23, §4.1)**

At sitting creation, the engine shall copy the learner's account\_mode (production or demo) into ExamAttempts.content\_tier so that all downstream item and task queries filter strictly on content\_tier \= ExamAttempts.content\_tier AND lifecycle\_status \= 'active' (D23) without branching into separate application code. Before serving the first item of a sitting that includes Listening, the engine shall execute the reused audio playback preflight check; before serving Speaking, the engine shall execute microphone permission and audio loopback preflight verification.

### **3.2 Serving Items, Stimulus Presentation, and Navigation Locks**

#### **FR-SERVE-01: Randomized Stratified Stage 1 Module Assembly (D3, D9, D11)**

For each examined objective skill (Listening, Reading), the sitting engine shall assemble Stage 1 (AttemptStageBundles.stage\_number \= 1\[span\_35\](start\_span)\[span\_35\](end\_span)) at sitting start by selecting active placement\_eligible \= true items stratified across CEFR bands and distinct topic\_id values. To eliminate the legacy defect of byte-identical repeat tests (§2.3 of the brief), the item selector shall exclude all item\_id and stimulus\_id identifiers encountered by the learner across their preceding 5 placement sittings and apply uniform cryptographic random sampling within each target (skill, item\_level, topic\_id) cell.

#### **FR-SERVE-02: Shared Stimulus Presentation and Local Independence Enforcement**

When serving objective items linked to a shared parent stimulus in StimulusGroups, the engine shall enforce modality-specific layout and acoustic rules. In Listening, audio playback is strictly linear (max\_plays \= 1\) with timeline scrubbing disabled; no item stem or option may reference a numeric timestamp, and all child items attached to the active audio track are rendered simultaneously on screen during a 15-second pre-audio reading window, throughout audio playback, and during the post-audio selection window, ordered chronologically by acoustic evidence (t\_{\\text{start}}(k) \< t\_{\\text{start}}(k+1)) with \\ge 8.0\\text{s} separating adjacent item evidence spans. In Reading, shared passages are rendered in a persistent scrollable pane alongside their child items, which are verified at ingestion to maintain character-span Intersection-over-Union \\text{IoU} \< 0.15 to preserve psychometric local independence (Yen, 1993).

#### **FR-SERVE-03: Hybrid Multistage Navigation Policy — Intra-Stage Revision vs. Inter-Stage Locks (§4.2.8)**

Section 4.2.8 of the brief asks whether the mock exam's one-way locks belong in a measurement instrument, noting that preventing a learner from correcting a mis-click alters the measurement. Psychometric research on computer-adaptive and multistage testing demonstrates that prohibiting item review induces test anxiety and contaminates ability estimates with clerical errors: across 75 empirical studies of answer revision (Waddell & Blankenship, 1994; Vispoel, 1998; Stone & Lunz, 1994), 57% to 67% of examinees revise answers when permitted, and **wrong-to-right corrections outnumber right-to-wrong changes by more than 2.5 to 1 (57\\% vs. 21\\%)**, significantly improving construct validity. Indeed, permitting intra-module answer review while locking completed stages is one of the primary psychometric advantages of Multistage Testing (MST) over item-level CAT (Hendrickson, 2007; Yan, von Davier, & Lewis, 2014; Zheng & Chang, 2015). Consequently, the placement engine replaces blanket item-level one-way locks with a **Four-Rule MST Navigation Policy**:

| Navigation Boundary | Modality / Section | Lock Behavior in Placement Instrument | Psychometric & Measurement Justification |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Intra-Stage Reading** | Reading Stage 1 & Reading Stage 2 | **Free Bidirectional Navigation:** Learners may freely move forward and backward across all items and passages within the currently active stage bundle before submitting the stage. | Allows candidates to correct mis-clicks and re-verify passage details without altering adaptive routing, reducing measurement error (Vispoel, 1998; Hendrickson, 2007). |
| **Intra-Stage Listening** | Listening Stage 1 & Listening Stage 2 | **One-Way Audio Playback \+ Free Option Revision Within Stage:** Audio tracks play once linearly without replay, but learners may revise their selected option (A–D) on any item within the active stage before stage submission. | Preserves real-time acoustic decoding validity (Field, 2008\) while ensuring an accidental mis-click on an earlier item in the same stage can be corrected before routing. |
| **Inter-Stage Boundary** | Stage 1 \\to Stage 2 Transition (L & R) | **Hard One-Way Lock on Stage 1 Submission:** Once Stage 1 is confirmed, AttemptStageBundles\[stage=1\].status becomes 'locked' permanently; URL tampering cannot reopen Stage 1\. | Stage 2 item difficulty reveals whether the candidate routed high or low; allowing backward edits into Stage 1 after seeing Stage 2 violates MST routing invariance (Yan et al., 2014). |
| **Productive Tasks** | Speaking (Tasks 1–2) & Writing (Tasks 1–2) | **Hard One-Way Lock per Task Submission:** Speaking Task 1 locks when its 60s recording ends (D19); Writing Task 1 locks upon submission to trigger in-flight AI grading (§4.2.7). | Enforces standardized 60-second oral production conditions (D19) and prevents mutating a free response while the asynchronous AI grader is already evaluating it (M1). |

### **3.3 Answering, Offline Resilience, and Reload Recovery**

#### **FR-ANS-01: Optimistic Local Persistence and Sequenced Draft Synchronization**

Every option click (A–D) on an objective item and every debounced text change (every 2,000ms or 15 characters typed) in a Writing task shall immediately write to client IndexedDB with a monotonically increasing client\_seq\_no and dispatch an asynchronous upsert to AttemptItemResponses or FreeResponses, refreshing ExamAttempts.last\_activity\_at.

#### **FR-ANS-02: Offline Operation, Dual-State Answered Count, and Reconnection Sync (§4.2.3)**

At the beginning of each stage bundle, the client pre-fetches all stimuli, item stems, options, and audio media for that active stage. If network connectivity drops mid-sitting, the engine displays a persistent non-modal banner (Connection lost — you can continue \[span\_137\](start\_span)\[span\_137\](end\_span)working in this section; answers are saved on your device) while permitting the candidate to continue answering cached items in the active stage or composing the active Writing draft. To prevent candidate confusion regarding unsynced work (§4.2.3), the section progress header shall display the **optimistic local answered count** (Answered: 7 of 8\) accompanied by an explicit sync status pill (2 saved locally · waiting for connection). If the candidate reaches a hard server-dependent boundary while offline—specifically the Stage 1 \\to Stage 2 routing boundary, a Speaking audio blob upload, or final sitting submission—the engine pauses transition and presents a reconnection retry modal. When connectivity returns, the client flushes queued mutations ordered by client\_seq\_no (where highest client\_seq\_no wins per item) and clears the unsynced indicator upon receiving server acknowledgment.

#### **FR-ANS-03: Resume After Browser Reload Across Objective, Recording, and Grading States (§4.2.2)**

Upon a page reload or browser crash-restart during an active sitting, the engine reconciles local IndexedDB state with the server's ExamAttempts record and restores the exact active stage and item pointer without advancing the item index. If the reload occurred during a Listening audio track, playback resumes from the last checkpointed 2-second media offset. If the reload occurred **during an active Speaking recording** (re\[span\_42\](start\_span)\[span\_42\](end\_span)cording\_started\_at IS NOT NULL AND status \= 'draft'), the incomplete audio stream cannot be spliced cleanly without prosodic artifacts; the engine discards the partial stream, increments FreeResponses.reload\_retry\_count (0 \-\> 1), and grants **exactly one reload recovery retry** on that task with the 60-second recording timer (D19) re-initialized. If a second reload occurs during recording on the same Speaking task (reload\_retry\_count \>= 1), to prevent candidates from repeatedly refreshing the browser to rehearse an already-revealed prompt, the engine automatically finalizes and submits buffered audio chunks if at least 15.0 seconds of speech were captured, or else replaces the task with an unseen sibling task\_id from the same (skill, task\_level, topic\_id) cell with zero remaining retries. Finally, if a reload occurs **during in-flight AI grading** of an already-submitted Speaking or Writing response (FreeResponses.status IN ('pending\_ai', 'graded'))—whether the learner is working on a subsequent task or viewing the progressive results screen—the reload is a pure read operation that never re-dispatches the AI grading job or resets task status; the client simply re-subscribes to the grading status stream.

### **3.4 The Two-Stage Transition and Productive Task Routing**

#### **FR-TRANS-01: Seamless Stage 1 to Stage 2 Transition and Atomic Interruption Point (§4.2.5)**

When the learner completes the final item of Stage 1 in Listening or Reading and clicks **Continue to Part 2**, the engine displays a confirmation dialog naming any unanswered Stage 1 items ("You have answered X of Y questions in Part 1\. Once you continue to Part 2, you cannot return to change these answers."). Upon confirmation, the server executes Stage 1 locking, Stage 1 scoring, and Stage 2 bundle selection inside a **single atomic database transaction** (AttemptStageBundles\[stage=1\].status \= 'locked' and AttemptStageBundles\[stage=2\].status \= 'active'). On the client, the learner experiences a seamless inline transition (\< 300ms skeleton state labeled "Loading Part 2...") that advances directly to the first item of Stage 2 without displaying any interim score, routing notice, or full-page reload. Because the transition commits atomically on the server, there is an exact, deterministic resume boundary: if the learner closes the tab or loses power prior to transaction commit, resuming returns them to the final item of Stage 1; if they are interrupted at any millisecond after commit, resuming places them directly on **Item 1 of Stage 2** with their routed Stage 2 item bundle locked in.

#### **FR-TRANS-02: Deterministic Stage 2 Routing Execution (D9, D10, D11)**

Inside the Stage 1 \\to Stage 2 transition transaction, the engine evaluates the candidate's Stage 1 response vector using the deterministic cumulative number-correct (cNC) and contiguous lower-band pass rules defined in Section 5.2 (Svetina et al., 2019; Luecht et al., 2006). The engine routes the candidate into one of five adjacent-band Stage 2 boundary modules (Module 2A \[A1, A2\], Module 2B \[A2, B1\], Module 2C \[B1, B2\], Module 2D \[B2, C1\], or Module 2E \[C1, C2\]), assembling at least 5 unseen placement\_eligible \= true items per band (D10) stratified across topics.

#### **FR-TRANS-03: Productive Task Selection Anchored to Objective Estimate (D6)**

Per Decision D6, Speaking and Writing administer **2 tasks per skill** selected to match the candidate's estimated proficiency level so that an A2 learner is never asked to compose an abstract C1 academic argument. When the sitting includes Listening and/or Reading (or reuses valid L/R results from the past 90 days per D18), the engine computes the conservative anchor band L\_{\\text{anchor}} \= \\min(\\text{CEFR}\_L, \\text{CEFR}\_R) (mapping Pre-A1 to A1) and selects an adjacent two-band productive pair \[L\_{\\text{low}}, L\_{\\text{high}}\] (\[A1, A2\] for L\_{\\text{anchor}}=\\text{A1}; \[A2, B1\] for \\text{A2}; \[B1, B2\] for \\text{B1}; \[B2, C1\] for \\text{B2}; \[C1, C2\] for \\text{C1} or \\text{C2}). To maximize multi-task dependability across person-by-task genre interactions (\\Phi \= 0.71; Brennan, 2001), Task 1 is drawn at L\_{\\text{low}} from an interactive or transactional/descriptive genre (S-DESC or S-INT in Speaking; W-CORR or W-DAT\[span\_139\](start\_span)\[span\_139\](end\_span)A in Writing) and Task 2 is drawn at L\_{\\text{high}} from an analytical or argumentative genre (S-SYN or S-ARG in Speaking; W-ARG or W-DISC in Writing).

### **3.5 Submitting, Partial Submission, and Inactivity Abandonment**

#### **FR-SUB-01: Partial Submission Confirmation and Unanswered Item Scoring (§4.2.4)**

Placement adopts and extends the mock exam's explicit confirmation behavior for partial submissions. ## **Section 4: Psychometric Business Rules, Scoring Mechanics, and Legacy Migration**

### **4.1 Objective Band Classification, the Guessing Floor, and the Monotonicity Rule**

In a 4-option multiple-choice test, random guessing yields an expected floor of c \= 0.25 correct responses. Under Decision D10, Stage 2 delivers at least 5 items per evaluated band so that a single lucky guess cannot swing accuracy by 25%, and under Decision D14, a candidate is awarded CEFR band L only if they achieve **\\ge 70\\% correct at band L and at every evaluated lower band**.  
Because item counts per band are discrete integers, the exact number of items n\_L \= n\_{1,L} \+ n\_{2,L} evaluated at band L across Stage 1 and Stage 2 exerts a profound non-linear effect on classification accuracy under D14's \\ge 70\\% rule. Under a binomial error model where a non-master of band L has true ability \\theta\_{\\text{low\[span\_62\](start\_span)\[span\_62\](end\_span)}} \= 0.20 (yielding an expected observed accuracy p\_0 \= 0.20 \+ 0.80(0.25) \= 0.40 with guessing) and a true master of band L has true ability \\theta\_{\\text{high}} \= 0.733 (yielding expected observed accuracy p\_1 \= 0.733 \+ 0.267(0.25) \= 0.80 per §3.3), evaluating n\_L \= 5 items requires 4/5 (80.0\\%) to pass, yielding false-promotion error \\alpha \= 8.70\\% and false-demotion error \\beta \= 26.27\\%. However, if a band is evaluated on n\_L \= 6 items (as occurred at A2 and C1 in the 1+5 baseline of §6.1), achieving \\ge 70\\% requires 5/6 (83.3\\%) correct—which spikes false demotion of true masters to \\beta \= 34.46\\%\! Conversely, equalizing the combined Stage 1 \+ Stage 2 evaluation to **n\_L \= 7 items per routed band** aligns the discrete passing threshold at **5/7 \= 71.43\\%**, keeping pure-guessing false passes at 1.29\\%, false promotion at \\alpha \= 9.63\\% (\<10\\%), and reducing false demotion to \\beta \= 14.80\\%.  
In a two-stage adaptive sitting, the candidate's routed Stage 2 boundary pair \[L\_{\\text{low}}, L\_{\\text{high}}\] is evaluated with high precision (n\_L \= 7 items per band in the equalized design, or 5\\text{–}7 items in the baseline), whereas bands strictly below L\_{\\text{low}} were sampled only thinly in Stage 1 (1\\text{–}2 items per band) to route the learner upward. To enforce D14 (\\ge 70\\% at band L and all lower bands) and D15 (flagging and truncating non-monotonic results) without allowing a single careless Stage 1 slip on an easy item to zero out a high-proficiency learner, the scoring engine evaluates the **Four-Case Band Mastery and Monotonicity Matrix** below:

### **4.2 Unified 0–100 Intra-Band Position Formula Across All Four Skills**

Decisions D12, D13, and D16 require every skill to report a 0–100 integer representing the learner's position inside their awarded CEFR band, computed on a unified unit scale so that all four skills can be compared directly to identify the weakest skill. This is achieved by mapping every skill onto the continuous CEFR proficiency continuum \\theta\_{\\text{CEFR}} \\in \[0.00, 6.99\], where the integer part k \\in \\{0..6\\} indexes the discrete CEFR band (0 \= Pre-A1, 1 \= A1, 2 \= A2, 3 \= B1, 4 \= B2, 5 \= C1, 6 \= C2) and the two-digit fractional part defines the intra-band position P \\in \[0, 100\].  
For the objective skills (Listening and Reading), once the discrete band L (with index k) is resolved via the D14/D15 matrix, the candidate's intra-band position P\_{\\text{obj}} \\in \[0, 100\] combines their excess mastery above the 70\\% threshold at band L (p\_L \\in \[0.70, 1.00\], contributing 70\\% of the intra-band span) with their progress toward the 70\\% threshold on the next higher band L+1 (p\_{L+1} \\in \[0.00, 0.70), contributing the remaining 30\\% of the span):  
P\_{\\text{obj}} \= \\text{round}\\left( 100 \\times \\left\[ 0.70 \\left( \\frac{\\text{clamp}(p\_L, 0.70, 1.00) \- 0.70}{0.30} \\right) \+ 0.30 \\left( \\frac{\\text{clamp}(p\_{L+1}, 0.00, 0.70)}{0.70} \\right) \\right\] \\right)  
For Pre-A1 (k=0, where p\_{\\text{A1}} \< 0.70), position inside Pre-A1 is P\_{\\text{obj}} \= \\text{round}(100 \\times p\_{\\text{A1}} / 0.70), and for C2 (k=6, where no higher band exists), P\_{\\text{obj}} \= \\text{round}(100 \\times (\\text{clamp}(p\_{\\text{C2}}, 0.70, 1.00) \- 0.70) / 0.30). Under this continuous formulation, a candidate who barely passes B2 (p\_{\\text{B\[span\_66\](start\_span)\[span\_66\](end\_span)2}} \= 5/7 \= 0.714) and gets 1/7 (0.143) on C1 receives B2 · 9; a candidate who gets 7/7 (1.00) on B2 and 4/7 (0.571) on C1 reaches B2 · 94; and getting one more C1 item right (5/7 \= 0.714) promotes them across the boundary to C1 · 3, matching the exact behavior described in D12.

P\_{\\text{prod}} \= \\text{round}\\left( 100 \\times \\frac{M\_{\\text{skill}} \- B\_{\\text{low}}(L)}{B\_{\\text{high}}(L) \- B\_{\\text{low}}(L)} \\right)

### **4.3 Productive Task Design, Rubric Controls, and Two-Task Reliability Defense**

As required by Section 5.1.3 of the brief, the productive half of the placement paper must defend its task types, rubric architecture, single-task reliability, and two-task confidence claim. Under Generalizability Theory variance decomposition of second-language speaking and writing assessments (Brennan, 2001; Lee & Kantor, 2005; Schoonen, 2005; §5.3), true candidate proficiency accounts for \\sigma^2\_p \= 55\\% of total score variance, while person-by-task genre interaction accounts for \\sigma^2\_{\[span\_69\](start\_span)\[span\_69\](end\_span)pt} \= 27\\% and task/rater residual error accounts for \\sigma^2\_t \+ \\sigma^2\_e \= 18\\%. Consequently, **a single productive task (n\_t \= 1\) achieves an absolute dependability index of only \\Phi(1) \= \\frac{0.55}{0.55 \+ 0.45/1} \= \\mathbf{0.550} (E\\rho^2 \= 0.567, \\text{S\[span\_149\](start\_span)\[span\_149\](end\_span)EM} \= \\pm 0.67 CEFR bands)**, meaning a single prompt cannot reliably distinguish adjacent CEFR levels. By administering **n\_t \= 2 tasks sampled from distinct communicative genres (D6)**, person-by-task interaction variance is halved, raising absolute dependability to **\\Phi(2) \= \\frac{0.55}{0.55 \+ 0.45/2} \= \\mathbf{0.710} (E\\rho^2 \= 0.724, \\text{SEM} \= \\pm 0.47 CEFR bands)** and satisfying the \\ge 0.70 diagnostic placement threshold.  
To ensure that automated AI grading preserves this \\Phi \= 0.710 dependability without collapsing toward the middle bands (B1/B2) or rewarding verbose boilerplate, the grading worker enforces the five anti-compression and anti-bias mechanisms specified in §5.2: (1) explicit binary syntactic and lexical gate conditions for band promotion (e.g., Band 4 W\_GR requires \\ge 3 distinct subordinate clause types and \\ge 60\\% error-free T-units; Gao, 2026); (2) four separate, dimension-decoupled LLM calls at temperature T \= 0.20 that must extract verbatim JSON evidence quotes before emitting an integer score (Zhao et al., 2026); (3) direct prompt injection of the six Whisper-v3/prosody acoustic telemetry metrics for Speaking (speech\_rate\_wpm, articulation\_rate, silent\_pauses\_over\_1500ms\_per\_mi\[span\_71\](start\_span)\[span\_71\](end\_span)n, mean\_pause\_duration\_ms, asr\_confidence, gop\_phoneme\_posterior; Zechner et al., 2009); (4) hard penalty ceilings capping W\_TR and S\_FC at Band 2 (A2) if response length falls below 50\\% of minimum bounds and capping W\_LR and W\_CC at Band 3 (B1) if Guiraud's root type-token ratio or n-gram repetition indicates memorized padding; and (5) injection of three task-specific calibrated benchmark responses at Bands 2, 3, and 4 into every grading call.

### **4.4 Pool Depletion Fallback Cascade and Post-Pool Telemetry Quarantine**

When assembling Stage 1 or Stage 2 for a candidate who has taken multiple retakes (or in the smaller demo tier), if a target (skill, item\_level, topic\_id) cell contains zero unseen active placement\_eligible \= true items, the item selector executes a strict **Three-Step Same-Band Fallback Cascade**. First, it relaxes the topic\_id constraint and draws an unseen active placement\_eligible \= true item from another topic within the **exact same (skill, item\_level) band**. Second, if all placement\_eligible \= true items in that band have been seen by the learner, it draws an unseen active placement\_eligible \= false item from the practice reserve at the **exact same (skill, item\_level) band**. Third, if the learner has seen every active item in that band across the entire pool, it recycles the placement\_eligible \= true item at that exact item\_level with the oldest last\_se\[span\_153\](start\_span)\[span\_153\](end\_span)en\_at timestamp for that learner and logs a PoolExhaustionReuseWarning. Under no circumstances may the selector substitute an item from an adjacent CEFR band to fill a slot, as doing so would alter the band denominator n\_L and invalidate D14's \\ge 70\\% threshold.  
Furthermore, once the pool is live, background telemetry jobs evaluate every active item crossing N \= 30, 50, 100, 250 learner attempts and automatically flip lifecycle\_status to 'quarantined' if item-total point-biserial correlation drops below r\_{\\text{pbis}} \< 0.10 at N \\ge 50 (\< 0.00 at N \\ge 3\[span\_73\](start\_span)\[span\_73\](end\_span)0), if empirical pass rate among target-band learners falls outside p \\in \[0.30, 0.92\], if a single distractor attracts \> 45\\% of target-band learners while the key attracts \< 35\\%, or if productive task AI scores drift by \> 0.75 bands from candidate objective baselines ( §7.4). When a quarantined item is confirmed defective (retired\_defective), historical placement attempts containing that item are re-evaluated excluding the defective item under **Monotonic Learner Protection**, meaning a learner's skill band or intra-band position may be upgraded retroactively but is never downgraded.

### **5.1 Sizing the Paper Within the 60-Minute Limit: Stage 1 and Stage 2 Arithmetic**

Section 5.1.1 of the brief mandates that Stage 1 and Stage 2 item allocations be derived from a single **60.0-minute total sitting budget** (3,600 seconds across all four skills: **15.0 minutes Listening, 15.0 minutes Reading, 8.0 minutes Speaking, and 22.0 minutes Writing**) to separate adjacent CEFR bands at a controlled classification error rate.  
As established in Section 4.1, evaluating n\_L \= 7 total items per band across Stage 1 and Stage 2 is the psychometric sweet spot for D14's \\ge 70\\% threshold because 5/7 \= 71.43\\% lands directly on the 70\\% cutoff, yielding \< 10\\% false promotion (\\alpha \= 9.63\\%) and 14.80\\% false demotion (\\beta), whereas evaluating 6 items forces a 5/6 \= 83.3\\% passing bar that inflates false demotion of true masters to 34.46\\%. In the attached Content Specification ( §6.1), the baseline objective blueprint specified **16 items per skill**: a 6-item Stage 1 (0 A1 \+ 1 A2 \+ 2 B1 \+ 2 B2 \+ 1 C1 \+ 0 C2) followed by a 10-item Stage 2 (5 items at L\_low \+ 5 items at L\_high). While that 16-item baseline gives B1 and B2 exactly 2 \+ 5 \= 7 items, it omits A1 and C2 from Stage 1 entirely—conflicting with Decision D9 (*"Stage 1: fixed blueprint across all bands"*)—and leaves A2 and C1 with 1 \+ 5 \= 6 total items, exposing A2 and C1 candidates to the 83.3\\% discretization penalty.

> * In **Stage 2 (10 to 12 items, 9.0 minutes)**, the engine administers **6 items** at any routed boundary band that had 1 item in Stage 1 (A1, A2, C1, C2) and **5 items** at any routed boundary band that had 2 items in Stage 1 (B1, B2). Section 5.3 of the brief requires an explicit statement of which values in the placement output contract the downstream learning-path generator is meant to use. First, **curriculum level initialization and lesson selection must read each skill's individual skill\_results\[skill\].cefr\_level and band\_position, never overall\_result.overall\_cefr**. Because overall\_cefr is the weakest skill (D1), a candidate who scores B2 · 71 in Listening, B2 · 42 in Reading, A2 · 64 in Speaking, and B1 · 50 in Writing receives an overall\_cefr of A2. If the learning path initialized all four skills at overall\_cefr (A2), it would prescribe elementary A2 Listening and Reading lessons to a B2 listener/reader—recreating the exact pathology that Decision D1 was designed to prevent. Instead, the learning path must instantiate four modality tracks anchored at their respective per-skill CEFR coordinates (Listening at B2, Reading at B2, Speaking at A2, and Writing at B1).  
Second, **goal feasibility evaluation and weekly study-hour budgeting must consume continuous\_theta\_cefr (cefr\_level \+ band\_position) across all examined skills alongside overall\_cefr**. When determining whether a learner's target band is achievable before their target exam date given their weekly study hours (§1 of the brief), the feasibility engine calculates the required study-hour delta for each examined skill from its current (cefr\_level, band\_position) to the target band using the cumulative study-hour lookup table (§9.2), allocating a proportionally higher share of weekly practice sessions to the limiting skill (overall\_result.limiting\_skill, e.g., Speaking at A2 · 64\) while scheduling maintenance and advancement drills for stronger skills. overall\_result.over\[span\_101\](start\_span)\[span\_101\](end\_span)all\_cefr serves as the headline readiness summary and conservative minimum-band indicator.  
Third, **sub-skill topic\_priors and criterion\_profile must be consumed strictly as sequencing priors within the assigned skill band, never as certified topic mastery**. Because Binomial SPRT requires n\_{\\text{obs}} \= 15 items per (topic\_id, cefr\_level) cell to certify objective topic mastery at \\alpha \= \\beta \= 0.10 ( §3.3) and Generalizability Theory requires \[span\_243\](start\_span)\[span\_243\](end\_span)n\_t \= 3\\text{–}4 tasks to certify productive topic mastery at \\Phi \\ge 0.79\\text{–}0.83 ( §5.3), the 1 to 3 items seen per topic during a 60-minute placement sitting cannot certify mastery of any cell; instead, the learning path uses weak\_signal topics and weakest\_criterion to schedule the learner's first remedial lessons within their assigned CEFR band. Finally, under Progressive Rendering (D5), the learning path may generate a provisional objective study preview as soon as Listening and Reading reach skill\_status \= 'measured', and must lock in the full multi-skill study schedule once PlacementTests.status transitions to 'do\[span\_103\](start\_span)\[span\_103\](end\_span)ne'.

### **7.1 Detailed Analysis of Open Architectural Questions and Future v2 Accessibility Hooks**

In addition to the five numerical and pool-allocation questions summarized in the decision matrix above, three operational nuances warrant explicit documentation for engineering sign-off. First, regarding **Stimulus Granularity in Objective Placement (Gap 2 in Section 5.3)**, the Content Specification ( §6.3) groups Listening items into 2-item (A\[span\_247\](start\_span)\[span\_247\](end\_span)1–A2), 3-item (B1, B2, C2), and 4-item (C1) shared audio stimuli with zero standalone 1-item audio tracks. Because Stage 1 requires 1 item at A1, A2, C1, and C2, and Stage 2 requires 5 or 6 items per band (which is not divisible by 3 or 4), the sitting engine must be configured to allow **subset child-item delivery**—rendering only the selected child item\_id(s) from a parent StimulusGroups record during a placement stage while keeping unselected sibling items hidden for future practice sittings.  
Second, regarding **Speaking and Writing Prompt Timing Envelopes at C1–C2 (Gap 5 in Section 5.3)**, Decision D19 fixes a hard **60-second recording limit per Speaking task** across the entire placement subsystem, whereas the Content Specification ( §2.2, §4.1) specifies 90–120 seconds of recording time for C1–C2 Speaking tasks and 250–350 words (14–30 minutes) for C1–C2 Writing tasks. For placement sittings, D19's 60-second recording cap is authoritative: all placement\_eligible \= true Speaking prompts at C1 and C2 must be scoped so that their three sub-prompts can be addressed within 60 seconds of dense, fluent speech (165–185 wpm), and the AI grader's S\_FC length-penalty gate must evaluate completeness against the 60-second window rather than penalizing the candidate for stopping at 60 seconds.  
Third, per Section 6 of the brief, **Sensory and Neurodivergent Accessibility Accommodations** are excluded from v1 by owner decision, but the v1 schema should avoid painted-in corners that would block v2 support. Specifically, supporting a deaf or hard-of-hearing candidate in a 4-skill certificate (IELTS, TOEIC\_LR, VSTEP) in v2 will require adding \[span\_109\](start\_span)\[span\_109\](end\_span)exempt\_accommodated to the PlacementSkillResults.skill\_status enum so that Listening can be exempted from overall\_cefr without marking the sitting as an unmeasured failure, alongside an accommodation flag in the AI grading prompt for dyslexic candidates in Writing that decouples orthographic spelling errors from morphosyntactic range (W\_GR) and lexical precision (W\_LR) gates.

### **8.2 Architectural Synthesis**

Rebuilding the placement subsystem around the single-writer ExamAttempts sitting engine (M1), a unified A1–C2 content pool (D2, D3), a two-stage adaptive objective blueprint equalized to 7 evaluated items per routed band (D9, D10, D14), and a two-task cross-genre productive evaluation (D6, \\Phi \= 0.710) systematically eliminates all five structural failures of the legacy diagnostic. By decoupling canonical CEFR measurement from certificate-specific scale projection (D16), permitting intra-stage answer revision while locking inter-stage boundaries (§3.2.3), and deriving progressive placement snapshots idempotently as asynchronous AI grading completes (D5, D7, D17), the platform delivers a fault-tolerant, psychometrically defensible four-skill starting coordinate (cefr\_level \+ 0–100 band\_position) and a conservative weakest-skill overall benchmark within a 60-minute sitting.
