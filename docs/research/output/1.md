# **Unified Content Blueprint and Psychometric Authoring Specification for Multi-Certificate English Assessment**

## **1\. System Boundaries and Non-Goal Constraints**

### **1.1 Architectural Mandate and Operational Boundaries**

This specification establishes the single authoritative content architecture, psychometric level model, topic taxonomy, item and stimulus schemas, analytical grading rubrics, reconciled item volume tables, and quality assurance rules for a multi-certificate English exam-practice and placement platform. All downstream system components—specifically the 60-minute two-stage adaptive placement engine, the diagnostic learning-path and topic-mastery engine, and the content administration pipeline—consume the volume matrices and schemas defined in this document without independent recalculation.  
The content architecture serves five distinct certificate configurations from a single unified, certificate-agnostic content pool. Every assessment unit—whether an objective multiple-choice item, a shared passage or audio stimulus, or a productive free-response task—and every instructional lesson is authored and stored against three canonical primary coordinates: skill (listening, reading, speaking, writing), cefr\_level (A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2), and topic\_id. No item in the database is locked to a single certificate; any certificate whose examined modalities include a given skill dynamically draws from that skill's pool without requiring certificate-specific item tagging.

### **1.2 Explicit Non-Goals**

To maintain strict separation of concerns across sibling specifications, four adjacent architectural domains are excluded from this document. First, application user interface and client rendering—including screen layouts, timer animations, audio player scrubbing controls, and client-side state management—are governed by the front-end application specification. Second, spaced-repetition and learning-path scheduling algorithms, such as temporal decay constants, daily study-plan routing logic, and remediation sequencing, belong exclusively to the learning-path specification. Third, runtime adaptive routing estimators, including Item Response Theory (IRT) provisional ability updates and Bayesian posterior boundary selection during placement, are owned by the placement specification, which consumes the pool depth and item formats established in Section 6\. Fourth, commercial packaging, subscription tiers, paywalls, and pricing entitlements lie outside the content domain.

## **2\. The Operational Level Model**

### **2.1 Defining a CEFR Level Operational Construct (Question 1\)**

Decision C1 mandates that a Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR) level (A1 through C2) is attached directly to each item, eliminating arbitrary 1–5 internal difficulty scales that previously caused miscalibration. Under the socio-cognitive assessment framework established by Weir (2005), Khalifa and Weir (2009), and the *CEFR Companion Volume* (Council of Europe, 2020), asserting that *"this item is B1"* is not a subjective editorial impression; it is a falsifiable psychometric claim that the item satisfies three simultaneous, measurable constraints across its stimulus input, cognitive operation, and response options.  
The first constraint governs stimulus input complexity (the contextual parameter): the lexical profile, morphosyntactic complexity, propositional density, discourse length, and—for Listening—acoustic delivery rate of the input text or audio track must fall within the empirical envelope that a learner at the target CEFR band can decode under real-time or timed conditions (Freedle & Kostin, 1993; Field, 2008). The second constraint governs cognitive operation demand (the processing parameter): the mental operation required to link the question stem to the stimulus must align with the cognitive hierarchy of the target band, progressing from local lexical recognition at A1, sentence-level synonymous paraphrase at A2, and cross-sentence synthesis at B1, to multi-proposition bridging inference and pragmatic stance recognition at B2, implicit rhetorical evaluation at C1, and dense abstract synthesis with fine register discrimination at C2 (Khalifa & Weir, 2009; Buck & Tatsuoka, 1998). The third constraint governs distractor competing plausibility or output precision (the response parameter): for objective four-option multiple-choice items, difficulty is anchored by the semantic and lexical proximity of the three distractors to the correct key and stimulus text (Haladyna, Downing, & Rodriguez, 2002), whereas for productive tasks, the band is anchored by the communicative complexity of the prompt and the linguistic thresholds of the scoring rubric. Both human reviewers and automated AI validators must apply the deterministic thresholds in the **Operational CEFR Level Matrix** below before an item can be assigned a level label.

### **2.2 Modality Specificity of CEFR Labels (Question 2\)**

Each item in the database carries one atomic cefr\_level label (A1–C2) because each item belongs to a single skill. However, the operational rules that define that label are strictly skill-dependent (Council of Europe, 2020; Field, 2008). Defining an A2 Listening item using the exact same textual parameters as an A2 Reading item is a foundational validity error because the four language modalities impose distinct cognitive processing constraints on working memory.  
In receptive assessment, Listening and Reading diverge along the axis of transient acoustic versus self-paced visual processing. Spoken input is linear, ephemeral, and paced entirely by the speaker; the listener must segment continuous phonological streams in real time without orthographic word boundaries or the ability to regress to earlier clauses (Field, 2008; Buck, 2001). Written text remains static on the screen, permitting visual scanning, backtracking, and recursive parsing. Consequently, at every CEFR band from A1 to C2, Listening stimuli must maintain a lower syntactic subordination index (\\approx 15\\%\\text{–}20\\% shorter MLTU) and a higher concentration of high-frequency lexical tokens than Reading stimuli at the identical band, while introducing modality-specific acoustic variables: speech rate in words per minute (wpm), connected-speech phonological reductions (assimilation, elision, weak forms), prosodic stress, and conversational turn-taking.  
In productive assessment, Speaking and Writing diverge along the axis of real-time oral formulation versus planned orthographic composition. Under Skehan's (1998) cognitive processing model, Speaking requires instantaneous conceptualization and phonological articulation under severe time pressure (15\\text{–}60 seconds of preparation), whereas Writing permits recursive planning, syntactic monitoring, and lexical editing over 8\\text{–}30 minutes. Consequently, a B2 Speaking response is defined by sustained oral discourse flow, natural prosodic chunking, and effective self-repair despite occasional real-time morphosyntactic slips, whereas a B2 Writing response must demonstrate explicit paragraph-level cohesion, controlled hypotactic syntax, and accurate orthography.

### **2.3 Level Attachment for Shared Stimuli and Multi-Item Groups (Question 3\)**

When a reading passage or listening audio track supports multiple questions—such as a literal detail question, a global main-idea question, and a bridging inference question—both the parent stimulus (stimulus\_level) and each individual child item (item\_level) carry explicit CEFR labels in the schema, and all adaptive placement routing and topic-mastery calculations operate strictly on the child item\_level.  
This dual-level architecture resolves a classic psychometric tension in passage-based assessment (Freedle & Kostin, 1993; Khalifa & Weir, 2009): a B2 stimulus passage naturally contains explicit local propositions that can be queried at B1 via straightforward paraphrase alongside implicit cross-paragraph rhetorical relationships that can be queried at B2 or C1. However, the parent stimulus imposes a rigid structural ceiling and floor on the difficulty of its child items. An A2 notice of 130 words lacks the propositional density or rhetorical ambiguity required to support a valid C1 inference item, and a C1 academic text of 550 words imposes far too heavy a lexical decoding burden for an A2 learner even when the stem asks for an explicitly stated detail.  
To prevent stimulus-item mismatch, the authoring pipeline enforces the **\\pm 1 Band Envelope Rule**. Every parent stimulus is authored and validated against a single stimulus\_level L\_s \\in \\{\\text{A1}, \\text{A2}, \\text{B1}, \\text{B2}, \\text{C1}, \\text{C2}\\}. When stimulus\_level belongs to the basic user bands (\\{\\text{A1}, \\text{A2}\\}), every attached child item must carry the exact same level (L\_i \= L\_s), because basic stimuli cannot host higher-band items without degenerating into trick questions. When stimulus\_level belongs to the independent or proficient user bands (\\{\\text{B1}, \\text{B2}, \\text{C1}, \\text{C2}\\}), each child item carries its own item\_level L\_i determined by its cognitive operation and distractor subtlety, constrained strictly to at most one adjacent band from the parent stimulus:  
L\_i \\in \\{L\_s \- 1, \\; L\_s, \\; L\_s \+ 1\\}  
Furthermore, at least two-thirds (\\ge 66\\%) of the child items on any stimulus must match the parent stimulus\_level (L\_i \= L\_s). When the placement engine selects a stimulus bundle during Stage 2 to separate an adjacent boundary such as B1 versus B2, a single B2 passage hosting two B2 items and one B1 item feeds both boundary buckets simultaneously while preserving authentic multi-item passage flow.

### **2.4 Certificate Scale Alignment and Crosswalk Defense (Question 4\)**

The existing product labels a TOEIC Listening & Reading total score of 785–940 as "B2" internally. This internal mapping is psychometrically invalid and must be eliminated from all item-tagging, placement estimation, and mastery tracking logic.  
As established in Educational Testing Service's (ETS) foundational standard-setting study, *Mapping the TOEIC and TOEIC Bridge Tests on the CEFR* (Tannenbaum & Wylie, 2008; conducted with a 22-expert European panel using a modified Angoff procedure), ETS never mapped the combined 10–990 TOEIC Listening & Reading total score to CEFR bands. Instead, ETS established independent minimum cut scores per skill section: Listening requires at least 400/495 for B2 and 490/495 for C1, whereas Reading requires at least 385/495 for B2 and 455/495 for C1 (with the Reading C1 cut reaching only 45% panelist agreement rather than the two-thirds consensus required for other cuts). Summing the two section minimums (400 \+ 385 \= 785 for B2; 490 \+ 455 \= 945 for C1) yields the colloquial institutional reference range of 785–940, but a candidate scoring 450 in Listening (B2) and 335 in Reading (B1) achieves a 785 total while remaining below the B2 threshold in Reading. Moreover, TOEIC Listening, Reading, Speaking, and Writing do not measure CEFR C2; their item difficulty and score scales top out at C1. Similarly, VSTEP.3-5 (governed by Vietnam Ministry of Education and Training Decision 729/QĐ-BGDĐT) scores each skill from 0.0 to 10.0 and maps 4.0–5.5 to Level 3 (B1), 6.0–8.0 to Level 4 (B2), and 8.5–10.0 to Level 5 (C1), awarding no certificate below 4.0 and providing no measurement at C2. Among the platform's five certificates, only IELTS officially spans C1 (Band 7.0–8.0) through C2 (Band 8.5–9.0).  
The architectural resolution therefore separates internal measurement from external score projection. Inside the engine, the item pool, placement estimator, and topic-mastery model operate exclusively on per-skill CEFR levels (A1–C2) with zero dependency on certificate scales. A defensible crosswalk is maintained solely at the learner-facing score report and target-setting presentation layer so that a learner's per-skill CEFR placement and mastery profile can be rendered as an estimated score range on their chosen certificate. Projected certificate scores must always be computed per skill from the learner's continuous ability estimate \\hat{\\theta}\_s within each CEFR band, and only combined into an overall certificate figure using that certificate's official aggregation rule—summing scaled section estimates for TOEIC Listening & Reading, averaging the four skills and rounding to the nearest 0.5 for IELTS and VSTEP, and reporting separate skill scores with no combined total for TOEIC Speaking & Writing.

## **3\. Topic Taxonomy and Mastery Grain**

### **3.1 Cross-Certificate Sharing and Orthogonal Axis Design (Questions 12 & 13\)**

To satisfy Decision C3 (*"One pool, all certificates"*), topics must be shared across all five certificates within each skill rather than defined per certificate. Defining "TOEIC Part 5 Verb Tense" separately from "IELTS Reading Grammar in Context" would fracture the item pool into certificate-specific silos, multiplying content authoring volume by five and preventing placement items or mastery history from transferring when a learner switches target certificates. Domain flavor—distinguishing workplace/business settings from academic or everyday general contexts—is instead captured via a non-structural metadata attribute (domain\_context: workplace \| academic \| general) balanced across every topic cell.  
Furthermore, topic\_id and cefr\_level operate as two strictly orthogonal axes (Topic × CEFR Level). "Verb tense at B2" is not a separate topic ID from "Verb tense at B1"; rather, the canonical topic is R-GRAM (*Morphosyntactic Structure and Lexical Precision in Context*) evaluated at cefr\_level \= "B2". Under cognitive diagnostic modeling (Tatsuoka, 1983; Buck & Tatsuoka, 1998\) and the CEFR curriculum spiral (Council of Europe, 2020), a single communicative micro-skill develops vertically across proficiency bands. Keeping topic\_id orthogonal to cefr\_level enables the learning-path engine to diagnose that a learner has mastered R-INF (*Bridging Inference*) at B1 while remaining weak in R-INF at B2, and to prescribe the exact (R-INF, B2) instructional lesson without creating 6 \\times K disconnected topic silos.

### **3.2 Complete Topic Taxonomy per Skill (Question 10\)**

The legacy system declared 15 topic categories and left 12 completely empty because its categories were arbitrary surface labels lacking cognitive boundaries. Grounded in Khalifa and Weir's (2009) cognitive hierarchy of reading, Field's (2008) cognitive validity model of listening, and Bachman and Palmer's (1996) communicative language ability framework for productive skills, the unified taxonomy defines **20 canonical topics across the four skills**: 6 in Listening, 6 in Reading, 4 in Speaking, and 4 in Writing. This grain size balances diagnostic resolution against sample efficiency: fewer than 4 to 6 topics per skill conflates distinct cognitive failures (such as confusing lexical decoding failure with pragmatic inference failure), whereas more than 8 topics per skill fragments learner observations across too many buckets, delaying statistical mastery convergence.

### **3.3 Psychometric Derivation of Item Count per Topic for Mastery (Question 11\)**

Determining how many items a topic requires before mastery can be judged—and how many unseen items must reside in each (topic\_id, cefr\_level) cell—requires applying Binomial Criterion-Referenced Mastery Testing and Wald's (1947) Sequential Probability Ratio Test (SPRT) (Ferguson, 1969; Macready & Dayton, 1977). For four-option multiple-choice items with a random guessing floor c \= 0.25, a non-master of a topic at band L has true latent knowledge \\theta\_{\\text{low}} \\le 0.333, yielding an expected observed proportion correct of at most p\_0 \= \\theta\_{\\text{low}} \+ (1 \- \\theta\_{\\text{low}})c \= 0.333 \+ 0.667(0.25) \= \\mathbf{0.50}. Conversely, a master of that topic at band L has true latent knowledge \\theta\_{\\text{high}} \\ge 0.733, yielding an expected observed proportion correct of at least p\_1 \= \\theta\_{\\text{high}} \+ (1 \- \\theta\_{\\text{high}})c \= 0.733 \+ 0.267(0.25) \= \\mathbf{0.80}.  
To discriminate between the null hypothesis H\_0: p \\le 0.50 (non-mastery) and the alternative hypothesis H\_1: p \\ge 0.80 (mastery) with Type I false-mastery error rate \\alpha and Type II false-non-mastery error rate \\beta, the minimum number of observed items n\_{\\text{obs}} under the normal approximation is:  
n\_{\\text{obs}} \= \\left( \\frac{z\_{\\alpha}\\sqrt{p\_0(1-p\_0)} \+ z\_{\\beta}\\sqrt{p\_1(1-p\_1)}}{p\_1 \- p\_0} \\right)^2  
At standard formative mastery precision (\\alpha \= 0.10, \\beta \= 0.10 \\implies z\_{0.10} \= 1.282, providing 90\\% classification accuracy at the boundary thresholds), substituting p\_0 \= 0.50 and p\_1 \= 0.80 yields:  
n\_{\\text{obs}} \= \\left( \\frac{1.282\\sqrt{0.50(0.50)} \+ 1.282\\sqrt{0.80(0.20)}}{0.80 \- 0.50} \\right)^2 \= \\left( \\frac{0.641 \+ 0.513}{0.30} \\right)^2 \= (3.846)^2 \= 14.79 \\approx \\mathbf{15\\text{ items}}  
With a criterion cut score of 11/15 correct (73.3\\%), a fixed window of 15 items classifies mastery with \\le 10\\% false-positive or false-negative error (whereas tightening \\alpha \= \\beta \= 0.05 with z\_{0.05} \= 1.645 requires n\_{\\text{obs}} \= 24.35 \\approx \\mathbf{25\\text{ items}}). When administered sequentially via Wald's SPRT in 5-item practice drills, clear masters (5/5 or 9/10 correct) and clear non-masters (\\le 2/5 correct) terminate early at 5 to 10 items, while borderline learners terminate at 15 items.  
However, the pool stock requirement per (topic\_id, cefr\_level) cell (N\_{\\text{cell}}) must exceed a single 15-item observation window so that a learner never encounters repeated items across remediation cycles. A learner diagnosed as weak in a topic after an initial 5-item diagnostic drill must complete a remedial lesson, take a 5-item post-lesson practice drill, complete a 5-item spaced review drill, and pass a 10-to-15-item mastery verification window—requiring 25 to 30 unseen items across sessions. Consequently, in the Full Production Pool, every (topic\_id, cefr\_level) cell across the high-density core bands (A2, B1, B2, C1) holds **30 objective items** (supporting 6 unseen 5-item drills or 2 full 15-item fixed mastery checks), and peripheral bands (A1, C2, per Decision C2) hold **15 objective items** (supporting 3 unseen 5-item drills or 1 full 15-item mastery check). For productive skills (Speaking and Writing), where responses are scored analytically across four continuous criteria without a 25\\% guessing floor, Generalizability Theory (Section 5.3) establishes that 3 to 4 tasks yield a dependability index \\Phi \\ge 0.79\\text{–}0.83. To allow a learner to practice, receive AI rubric feedback, study a lesson, and re-verify without repeating a prompt, each (topic\_id, cefr\_level) cell holds **8 tasks at A2–C1** (4 unseen 2-task sessions) and **4 tasks at A1 and C2** (2 unseen 2-task sessions).

### **4.2 Hard Shape 1: Listening with Shared Audio (Question 15\)**

One audio asset (stimulus\_id) serves a group of child items (items\[\]) to eliminate audio duplication and mirror authentic multi-item listening sections across TOEIC, IELTS, and VSTEP. Audio duration, word count, and child item count are strictly governed by the parent stimulus\_level as summarized in the table below.  
During test or practice administration, no item stem or option may reference or depend on a numeric timestamp (for example, asking *"At 0:42, why does the speaker pause?"* is strictly prohibited), nor may an item require the learner to scrub the audio player. In authentic TOEIC, IELTS, and VSTEP administrations, audio plays linearly from start to finish. When an L-ATT or L-FUNC item evaluates the pragmatic meaning of a specific utterance, the stem must quote the short phrase verbatim (e.g., *"Why does the woman say, 'That might be optimistic'?"*) or anchor the question by speaker turn and conversational topic.  
To preserve cognitive validity during linear playback, child items (sequence\_index: 1, 2, 3, 4\) must follow the exact chronological order in which their acoustic evidence occurs in the recording (evidence\_span.start\_sec of Item k \< evidence\_span.start\_sec of Item k+1), with the sole exception of global L-GIST items which must sit at sequence\_index \= 1\. Furthermore, the evidence spans of any two adjacent detail or inference items must be separated by at least **8.0 seconds** of audio so the candidate has time to process Item k before the acoustic cue for Item k+1 is spoken. While the question stem cannot depend on a timestamp during testing, every child item must populate "evidence\_span": {"start\_sec": float, "end\_sec": float, "verbatim\_quote": string} inside its explanation metadata so the post-submission review interface can jump directly to the relevant audio segment.

### **4.3 Hard Shape 2: Reading with Shared Passage (Question 16\)**

For any single task t, the AI grader outputs four integer criterion scores c\_1, c\_2, c\_3, c\_4 \\in \\{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6\\}, yielding an unweighted task composite mean M\_t \= \\frac{1}{4}\\sum\_{j=1}^4 c\_j \\in \[0.0, 6.0\]. ### **5.2 Rubric Engineering to Prevent Central-Tendency Compression (Question 18\)**

Empirical evaluations of LLM-as-a-Judge in L2 writing and speaking assessment demonstrate that direct holistic prompting produces severe central-tendency compression—collapsing 80% of scores into B1/B2 and shrinking automated score variance to 0.24 compared to human rater variance of 0.57 (Wang et al., 2017)—alongside length bias that rewards verbose B1 responses with B2 or C1 scores. To guarantee sharp discrimination between adjacent CEFR bands, the grading specification enforces five structural mechanisms across all productive tasks.  
First, every band transition (k-1 \\to k) in Section 5.1 is engineered as a set of necessary-and-sufficient binary gate conditions (for instance, Band 4 W\_GR requires \\ge 3 distinct complex subordinate clause types and \\ge 60\\% error-free T-units). As demonstrated by Gao (2026), converting LLM essay grading from direct scalar rating to threshold-style binary observations across band gates increases exact human-LLM agreement from 28.46\\% to 67.03\\%, lowers Mean Absolute Deviation from 0.2283 to 0.0837, and achieves 97.4\\%\\text{–}98.8\\% adjacent-band precision. Second, following Zhao et al. (2026), the AI grader executes dimension-decoupled evaluation at low temperature (T \= 0.20), evaluating each of the four criteria (TR/FC, CC/PR, LR, GR) independently with structured JSON evidence extraction—quoting specific error-free clauses, cohesive transitions, and errors before emitting the integer score 0\\text{–}6—to prevent halo carryover across dimensions.  
Third, because a text LLM grading a raw speech transcript is blind to pronunciation, intonation, and pause timing, the Speaking grading pipeline follows ETS SpeechRater architecture (Zechner et al., 2009; Wang et al., 2017\) and modular audio-LLM pipelines (Hirschi et al., 2025; Parikh et al., 2026\) by extracting and injecting six acoustic telemetry parameters directly into the grading prompt alongside the verbatim ASR transcript: speech\_rate\_wpm, articulation\_rate\_syllables\_per\_sec, silent\_pauses\_over\_1500ms\_per\_min, mean\_pause\_duration\_ms, asr\_word\_confidence\_mean, and gop\_phoneme\_posterio\[span\_103\](start\_span)\[span\_103\](end\_span)r\_score. Fourth, hard length-and-repetition ceiling rules cap W\_TR and S\_FC at 2 (A2) whenever a response's word count falls below 50\\% of the task minimum, and cap W\_LR and W\_CC at 3 (B1) whenever root type-token ratio (Guiraud's Index) or n-gram repetition reveals formulaic padding. Fifth, every productive task in the JSON pool embeds a task\_specific\_checklist (defining the exact three prompt requirements to verify for W\_TR or S\_FC) alongside three calibrated benchmark descriptors at Bands 2 (A2), 3 (B1), and 4 (B2) so the LLM judge evaluates the learner's response relative to concrete task anchors (Zhao et al., 2026; Gao, 2026).

### **5.3 Psychometric Reliability Claim for One AI-Scored Task and Multi-Task Requirements (Question 19\)**

A single AI-scored productive task (n\_t \= 1\) evaluated under the Section 5.1–5.2 rubric and anchor-threshold protocol achieves an inter-rater reliability (agreement between the AI judge and expert human consensus on that specific text or audio sample) of \\text{QWK} \\approx 0.72\\text{–}0.82 for Writing and \\text{QWK} \\approx 0.70\\text{–}0.78 for Speaking, satisfying the operational automated-scoring benchmark of \\text{QWK} \\ge 0.70 established by Williamson, Xi, and Breyer (2012) and Wang and von Davier (2014). **However, the score generalizability (dependability index \\Phi) of a single task (n\_t \= 1\) as a measure of a learner's true underlying skill proficiency is only \\Phi \\approx 0.55 (E\\rho^2 \\approx 0.57). Therefore, one task is psychometrically insufficient to assign a per-skill CEFR level; at least 2 tasks across distinct genres (\\Phi \= 0.71, E\\rho^2 \= 0.72) are required for Placement (Decision C5), and 3 to 4 tasks (\\Phi \= 0.79\\text{–}0.83, E\\rho^2 \= 0.80\\text{–}0.84) are required to certify skill or topic mastery in Practice.**  
This distinction between rater consistency on a single text and score generalizability across a skill domain is governed by Generalizability (G) Theory (Brennan, 2001; Lee & Kantor, 2005; Schoonen, 2005). Even when an LLM grader at T \= 0.20 drives within-task rater variance toward zero, the observed score variance \\sigma^2(X\_{pt}) of person p on task t decomposes into four components:  
\\sigma^2(X\_{pt}) \= \\sigma^2\_p \+ \\sigma^2\_t \+ \\sigma^2\_{pt} \+ \\sigma^2\_{e}  
where \\sigma^2\_p is true person ability variance, \\sigma^2\_t is fixed task-difficulty stringency variance, \\sigma^2\_{pt} is person-by-task interaction variance (reflecting the empirical reality that a learner performs differently on a functional email than on an abstract essay, or on a personal description than on a multi-option negotiation), and \\sigma^2\_e is residual/rater measurement error. Across empirical G-theory studies of L2 Speaking and Writing (Lee & Kantor, 2005; Schoonen, 2005; Attali & Burstein, 2006; Wang et al., 2017), standardized variance proportions average \\sigma^2\_p \= 0.55 (55\\%), \\sigma^2\_t \= 0.03 (3\\%), \\sigma^2\_{pt} \= 0.27 (27\\%), and \\sigma^2\_e \= 0.15 (15\\%). Because \\sigma^2\_{pt} \= 0.27 can only be reduced by sampling multiple tasks n\_t, the relative Generalizability Coefficient E\\rho^2(n\_t) and absolute domain-referenced Dependability Index \\Phi(n\_t) across n\_t tasks are given by:  
E\\rho^2(n\_t) \= \\frac{\\sigma^2\_p}{\\sigma^2\_p \+ \\frac{\\sigma^2\_{pt} \+ \\sigma^2\_e}{n\_t}} \= \\frac{0.55}{0.55 \+ \\frac{0.42}{n\_t}}, \\qquad \\Phi(n\_t) \= \\frac{\\sigma^2\_p}{\\sigma^2\_p \+ \\frac{\\sigma^2\_t \+ \\sigma^2\_{pt} \+ \\sigma^2\_e}{n\_t}} \= \\frac{0.55}{0.55 \+ \\frac{0.45}{n\_t}}

### **6.1 Arithmetic for Consumer 1: The 60-Minute Two-Stage Placement Sitting (Question 5a)**

Placement must operate within a hard limit of 60 minutes total across all four skills, with half the sitting allocated to objective skills and half to productive skills, using a two-stage adaptive design that separates adjacent CEFR bands. In the 30-minute objective half, **Listening** is allocated 15.0 minutes (at an average of 52\\text{–}56 seconds per item, amortizing 30\\text{–}90\\text{s} shared audio tracks across 2\\text{–}4 child items plus 15\\text{s} question preview and response time, allowing a candidate to complete exactly **16 Listening items**) and **Reading** is allocated 15.0 minutes (at an average of 55\\text{s} per item across shared passages and standalone cloze stems, allowing a candidate to complete exactly **16 Reading items**). In the 30-minute productive half, **Speaking** is allocated 8.0 minutes to administer **2 tasks** per Decision C5 (Task 1: core B1 transactional/descriptive prompt with 45\\text{s} preparation and 60\\text{s} recording; Task 2: routed boundary A2/B1 or B2/C1 negotiation/argumentation prompt with 60\\text{s} preparation and 90\\text{s} recording, plus microphone check and transitions), and **Writing** is allocated 22.0 minutes to administer **2 tasks** per Decision C5 (Task 1: functional correspondence or data synthesis over 8.0 minutes, 80\\text{–}130 words; Task 2: argumentative or evaluation essay over 14.0 minutes, 150\\text{–}220 words). The four sections sum to 15 \+ 15 \+ 8 \+ 22 \= \\mathbf{60.0\\text{ minutes}}.  
Within each objective skill (16 items total), the two-stage adaptive blueprint splits into a 6-item Stage 1 routing module and a 10-item Stage 2 boundary module. Stage 1 administers a fixed stratified blueprint across the core distribution to compute provisional ability \\hat{\\theta}\_1:  
\\text{Stage 1 Blueprint} \= 1\\text{ item (A2)} \+ 2\\text{ items (B1)} \+ 2\\text{ items (B2)} \+ 1\\text{ item (C1)} \= \\mathbf{6\\text{ items per objective skill}}\$\$In Listening, Stage 1 is delivered as one 2-item A2/B1 exchange, one 2-item B1/B2 dialogue, and one 2-item B2/C1 monologue; in Reading, Stage 1 is delivered as two standalone \`R-GRAM\` items at A2 and B1 plus one 4-item B2 passage containing one B1, two B2, and one C1 child item. Based on the Stage 1 score (\$0\\text{–}6\$), the learner is routed to one of the five adjacent CEFR boundary pairs: \`(A1, A2)\`, \`(A2, B1)\`, \`(B1, B2)\`, \`(B2, C1)\`, or \`(C1, C2)\`. Per Decision C4 (\*"Placement stage 2 needs at least 5 items per band for objective skills"\* to overcome the \$25\\%\$ guessing floor of 4-option items), Stage 2 administers \*\*5 items at the lower candidate band \$L\_{\\text{low}}\$ and 5 items at the upper candidate band \$L\_{\\text{high}}\$\*\*:\$\$\\text{Stage 2 Blueprint} \= 5\\text{ items }(L\_{\\text{low}}) \+ 5\\text{ items }(L\_{\\text{high}}) \= \\mathbf{10\\text{ items per objective skill}}  
Summing Stage 1 and Stage 2 reveals the exact maximum number of items a learner can encounter in any single CEFR band during one placement sitting: at A1 or C2, a learner sees 0\\text{ (Stage 1)} \+ 5\\text{ (Stage 2)} \= \\mathbf{5\\text{ items per skill}}; at A2 or C1, a learner sees 1\\text{ (Stage 1)} \+ 5\\text{ (Stage 2)} \= \\mathbf{6\\text{ items per skill}}; and at B1 or B2, a learner sees 2\\text{ (Stage 1)} \+ 5\\text{ (Stage 2)} \= \\mathbf{7\\text{ items per skill}}. To support initial onboarding placement, mid-course diagnostic re-placement, exit verification, and item exposure control (r\_{\\max} \\le 0.167) with zero item repeats across at least 6\\times parallel non-overlapping forms, the Placement Consumer requires 6 \\times 5 \= \\mathbf{30\\text{ items}} at A1 and C2, 6 \\times 6 \= \\mathbf{36\\text{ items}} at A2 and C1, and 6 \\times 7 \= \\mathbf{42\\text{ items}} at B1 and B2 (expanded to **60 placement-eligible items per band across A2–C1** in the unified table below so every one of the 6 topics contributes equally).

### **6.3 Reconciling Placement and Practice into One Unified Pool (Questions 5c, 6, 7\)**

Reconciling both consumers into a single shared pool without a learner ever meeting a placement item again during topic practice follows directly from the cell-level intersection arithmetic. In a single placement sitting, a candidate answers at most 5 to 7 objective items in any single CEFR band across all 6 topics combined—which consumes **at most 1 to 2 items in any individual (topic\_id, cefr\_level) cell**.  
Because the unified pool holds **30 items per (topic\_id, cefr\_level) cell** at A2–C1 (180\\text{ items/band/skill}) and **15 items per cell** at A1 and C2 (90\\text{ items/band/skill}), **10 items per topic cell at A2–C1** (60\\text{ items/band/skill}, providing 8.5 to 10 non-overlapping placement forms) and **5 items per topic cell at A1 and C2** (30\\text{ items/band/skill}, providing 6 non-overlapping placement forms) carry the flag "placement\_eligible": true. When a learner sits a placement exam, the placement engine draws exclusively from "placement\_eligible": true items and appends those administered item\_ids to the learner's exposure history (seen\_item\_ids). ## **7\. Authoring Pipeline, Acceptance Gates, and Lifecycle Rules**

### **7.1 End-to-End Authoring Pipeline and Cross-Model Separation (Question 20\)**

Per Decision C6, content is generated by AI, validated automatically through deterministic gates, and audited via a 10% stratified human review sample without a live pre-release pilot study. A critical operational risk highlighted in the brief is that the AI both authors content and grades learner responses. In the empirical literature on automated evaluators (Zheng et al., 2023; Panickssery et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2026), using the same model family to author prompts or reference answers and subsequently verify keys or grade student responses introduces two systemic distortions: narcissistic self-preference bias, wherein an LLM judge assigns inflated scores (+0.4\\text{ to }+0.8 bands) to responses that echo its own pre-training lexical priors while penalizing valid alternative human phrasing, and shared blind-spot propagation, wherein an authoring model's logical error in a distractor or prompt is shared by the identical model acting as validator. To eliminate both failure modes, the pipeline enforces **Strict Cross-Family Model Separation (Author\_LLM \\neq Verifier\_LLM \\neq Grader\_LLM)** across five sequential stages.

| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |

### **7.2 Automated Validation Gates (Question 21\)**

No item or task may advance from draft to validated—or enter the 10% human review sample—unless it passes 100% of the automated checks across four sequential validation gates.  
**Gate 1 (Structural and Haladyna Item-Writing Hygiene Gate)** enforces the empirical multiple-choice rules of Haladyna, Downing, and Rodriguez (2002). The validator verifies JSON schema compliance, non-empty explanations and distractor rationales, and four distinct options (A, B, C, D) with pairwise Levenshtein distance \> 2\. It rejects any item containing forbidden meta-options matching /(all\|none) of the above\|both \[a-d\] and \[a-d\]\|neither \[a-d\] nor \[a-d\]/i (Haladyna Guideline \#20). To eliminate longest-option key bias (Haladyna Guideline \#24), Gate 1 computes character length L(o\_k) and word count W(o\_k) for all four options and rejects any item where the correct option's word count deviates by \> 25\\% (or \> 4 words on short options) from the distractor mean, or any batch where the correct key is the strictly longest option in \> 30\\% of items. It further parses part-of-speech (POS) tags on option boundaries to guarantee syntactic parallelism, checks that no unique content lemma appears in both the stem and key while absent from the stimulus and distractors (Haladyna Guideline \#23), and enforces balanced key distribution (\[20\\%, 30\\%\] per letter A–D within each cell).  
**Gate 2 (Quantitative Linguistic and Acoustic Complexity Gate)** computes exact word count, Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level, Mean Length of T-Unit (MLTU via syntactic dependency parsing), clauses per T-unit, and target-band lexical coverage (percentage of tokens within the English Vocabulary Profile / Oxford 5000 lists for that band), rejecting any stimulus that falls outside the numeric bounds defined in Sections 2.1 and 2.2. For Listening stimuli, Gate 2 runs an independent Automatic Speech Recognition engine (Whisper-Large-v3) on the rendered audio file and rejects the asset if the Word Error Rate (\\text{WER}) against the source script exceeds 0.0\\% (preventing TTS mispronunciations or clipped words), if measured speech\_rate\_wpm falls outside the band envelope in Section 2.2, or if evidence\_span.start\_sec between adjacent items is less than 8.0 seconds.  
**Gate 3 (Blind Dual-Model Answer-Key and Stimulus-Dependency Gate)** protects the highest-risk artefact in the system: the answer key. Two heterogeneous verifier models (Verifier\_LLM\_1 from Model Family B and Verifier\_LLM\_2 from Model Family C, both distinct from Author\_LLM and running at T \= 0.0) are presented with the stimulus, stem, and four options while correct\[span\_26\](start\_span)\[span\_26\](end\_span)\[span\_31\](start\_span)\[span\_31\](end\_span)\_option and explanation are withheld. The item passes only if both verifiers independently select the exact correct\_option with token probability or self-consistency confidence \\ge 0.85 and assign \< 0.15 probability to every distractor. Furthermore, for all Listening and Reading comprehension items (excluding standalone R-GRAM), Gate 3 executes a **stimulus-blind ablation check** in which the solver receives only the stem and four options without the passage or transcript; if the solver identifies the key with probability \\ge 0.50 without the stimulus, the item is rejected for testing prior world knowledge or option implausibility rather than comprehension.  
**Gate 4 (Level-Consistency, Local Independence, and Synthetic Grader Calibration Gate)** verifies that an independent rubric classifier predicts the child item's cognitive operation and distractor proximity at \\hat{L}\_i \= L\_i \\in \\{L\_s \- 1, L\_s, L\_s \+ 1\\}, and confirms that evidence\_span character or timestamp intervals of sibling items on the same stimulus maintain overlap \\text{IoU} \< 0.15 (Yen, 1993). For Speaking and Writing tasks, Gate 4 generates six synthetic calibration responses spanning Bands 1 (A1) through 6 (C2)—including one adversarial "verbose B1 trap" (280 words of repetitive simple clauses) and one "concise C1 benchmark" (200 words of dense subordination)—and scores all six using the runtime Grader\_LLM against the Section 5 rubric. The productive task passes Gate 4 only if Grader\_LLM recovers the strict monotonic ordering (M\_1 \< M\_2 \< M\_3 \< M\_4 \< M\_5 \< M\_6), scores the verbose B1 trap \\le 3.25, and lands within \\pm 0.50 bands of every synthetic target.

| Validation Gate | Target Artefacts | Primary Quantitative / Psychometric Acceptance Threshold | Failure Action |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Gate 1: Structural & Haladyna** | Objective 4-MCQs | 4 options; 0 meta-options; key word count within \\pm 25\\% of distractor mean; POS parallelism; key balance 20\\%\\text{–}30\\% | Immediate auto-reject & regenerate |
| **Gate 2: Linguistic & Acoustic** | All Stimuli & Audio | Word count, FK Grade, MLTU, and lexical coverage inside Section 2 bounds; ASR \\text{WER} \= 0.0\\%; item gap \\ge 8.0\\text{s} | Immediate auto-reject & re-synthesize |
| **Gate 3: Blind Dual-Model Key** | Objective 4-MCQs | Verifier\_LLM\_1 and Verifier\_LLM\_2 both choose key (P \\ge 0.85); stimulus-blind ablation P(\\text{key}) \< 0.50 | Immediate auto-reject (zero key tolerance) |
| **Gate 4: Level & Grader Calibration** | All Items & Tasks | Predicted \\hat{L}\_i \= L\_i; sibling span \\text{IoU} \< 0.15; productive synthetic ladder strictly monotonic (M\_1 \< \\dots \< M\_6) | Auto-reject & regenerate prompt/checklist |

### **7.3 Human Review Protocol and Sampling Strategy (Question 22\)**

Per Decision C6, 10% of all items and tasks that pass Gates 1–4 are human-reviewed by qualified ELT assessment specialists before a generation batch is activated. Because simple random sampling across 2,120 units risks under-sampling C1/C2 or specific topics, the 10% sample is **stratified across every (skill, cefr\_level, topic\_id) cell**: in the Full Production Pool, the review queue samples 33 Listening audio bundles (99 items), 38 Reading stimuli (100 items), 16 Speaking tasks, and 16 Writing tasks (totaling 231 units, or 10.9\\% of the pool, with at least 1 bundle sampled per topic cell).  
During review, the human specialist first answers each objective item blind (without seeing the AI key). Under Lot Quality Assurance Sampling (LQAS) rules, a **zero-tolerance key rule** applies: if even a single answer-key error is discovered in the 10% human sample of a (skill, cefr\_level) stratum, 100% of that stratum is automatically quarantined and routed for full human key verification or regeneration. For non-key criteria (CEFR level drift exceeding \\pm 0.5 bands, unnatural distractors, or TTS prosodic artifacts), if \> 5\\% of sampled items in a batch stratum fail human review, the entire stratum is rejected and regenerated.

### **7.4 Item Rejection and Post-Pool Live Drift Remediation (Question 23\)**

An item is rejected prior to pool entry if it fails any check in Gates 1–4 or if a human reviewer identifies multiple defensible keys, a false key, a CEFR difficulty mismatch \\ge 1 band, factual inaccuracy, cultural bias, or robotic TTS prosody; rejected items are written to an immutable audit log (rejected\_artefacts\_log) for prompt diagnostics and never enter the items table.  
Because Decision C6 accepts the risk of unpiloted items entering the active pool, the runtime platform executes an automated **Live Item Telemetry Job** every time an active item crosses N \= 30, 50, 100, and 250 learner administrations. An active item is automatically flipped from lifecycle\_status \= "active" to "quarantined" (removing it immediately from all placement and practice selection queries) if it triggers any of four psychometric drift conditions:

> 1. **Point-Biserial Discrimination Collapse:** The item-total biserial correlation among learners at or near the item's target band falls below r\_{\\text{pbis}} \< 0.10 at N \\ge 50 (or r\_{\\text{pbis}} \< 0.00 at N \\ge 30, indicating that higher-ability learners prefer a distractor over the key).  
> 2. **Empirical Difficulty (p-value) Inversion:** Among learners placed at the item's target CEFR band, empirical proportion correct falls outside p \\in \[0.30, 0.92\] at N \\ge 50 (against an expected target-band p \\in \[0.55, 0.75\]).  
> 3. **Distractor Dominance:** A single distractor is selected by \> 45\\% of target-band learners while the key is selected by \< 35\\%.  
> 4. **Productive Task Bias Drift:** For a Speaking or Writing task at N \\ge 30, mean AI score deviates by \> 0.75 bands from the learners' established skill level (following the ETS 0.30 standardized mean difference flagging rule established by Wang & von Davier, 2014).

When a "quarantined" item is inspected by an SME and confirmed to have a defective answer key or broken stimulus, its status transitions to "retired\_defective", and an automated reconciliation job executes across all historical learner attempts referencing that item\_id: the defective item is excluded from the scoring denominator (or credited if the learner selected the true key), and **monotonic learner protection** guarantees that if recalculating the session upgrades a learner's placement band or flips a topic from non-mastered to mastered, the learner's profile is upgraded automatically, whereas no learner's level or mastery status is ever downgraded due to a content defect.

### **7.5 Non-Destructive Item Retirement Lifecycle (Question 24\)**

To retire an item without breaking learner history, analytics, or past test review screens, the database enforces **Soft-State Lifecycle Management with Immutable Versioning**. Foreign keys from learner\_attempt\_items, placement\_responses, and practice\_logs to items(item\_id, version) and stimuli(stimulus\_id, version) are declared with ON DELETE RESTRICT; no row in items, stimuli, productive\_tasks, or lessons may ever be removed via hard DELETE.  
Every content record carries lifecycle\_status \\in \\{\\text{draft}, \\text{validated}, \\text{active}, \\text{quarantined}, \\text{retired}, \\text{retired\\\_defective}\\}. Runtime selection queries for Placement and Practice assembly strictly filter WHERE lifecycle\_status \= 'active' AND content\_tier \= :session\_tier, ensuring that once an item transitions to quarantined, retired, or retired\_defective, no new test or practice session can draw it. Conversely, historical rendering queries for attempt review screens join directly on (item\_id, version) with no filter on lifecycle\_status (rendering a transparent notice badge if lifecycle\_status \= 'retired\_defective'), preserving the exact stimulus, stem, options, audio, and explanation for every past attempt. While cosmetic typo fixes in explanations update in place, any modification to a stimulus text, audio file, stem, option text, correct\_option, or item\_level requires retiring the existing version (lifecycle\_status \= 'retired') and inserting a new version so historical psychometric telemetry (p-value, r\_{\\text{pbis}}) is never contaminated.

### **7.6 Demo Set vs. Production Content Isolation (Questions 25 & 26\)**

Per Decision C8, the demo set is a 456-unit miniaturized run of the exact 120-cell matrix (5 objective items per (topic, level) cell \= 360 items; 2 productive tasks per (topic, level) cell \= 96 tasks; plus 120 lessons) generated by the real AI authoring pipeline and scored at runtime by the real AI grader.  
To guarantee that demo items can never be mistaken for production content and can be removed cleanly, the schema enforces **triple-layer data isolation**. First, every record in stimuli, items, productive\_tasks, and lessons carries a non-nullable indexed enum column "content\_tier": "demo" \| "production". Second, deterministic ID prefix namespaces require demo artefacts to match ^(stim\|item\|task\|les)\_demo\_ and production artefacts to match ^(stim\|item\|task\|les)\_prod\_, allowing runtime session validators to reject any cross-tier payload immediately. Third, demo records carry "batch\_tag": "seed-demo-v1". Because user accounts carry account\_mode \= 'demo' \| 'production', runtime selection queries bind content\_tier \= account.account\_mode. To decommission the demo set in a shared environment where demo attempt logs are retained for audit, executing UPDATE items SET lifecycle\_status \= 'retired' WHERE content\_tier \= 'demo' (and identically on stimuli and productive\_tasks) removes 100% of demo content from active selection in under 10 milliseconds without foreign-key violations; in a pre-launch staging purge, deleting demo sandbox attempts (DELETE FROM learner\_attempts WHERE account\_mode \= 'demo') followed by DELETE FROM items WHERE content\_tier \= 'demo' removes every demo record cleanly via the indexed partition.

### **8.2 Open Question 2: Full-Length Certificate Mock Exam Stock vs. Shared Practice Pool**

Section 3 of the brief notes a critical defect in the legacy database: *"the IELTS mock declares 40 items and holds 2."* A full timed certificate mock requires substantial item counts in a single sitting: IELTS requires 40 Listening \+ 40 Reading items \+ 2 Writing \+ 3 Speaking parts; VSTEP requires 35 Listening \+ 40 Reading items \+ 2 Writing \+ 3 Speaking parts; and TOEIC Listening & Reading requires 100 Listening \+ 100 Reading items. **Recommendation:** Adopt **Option A (Dynamic Unseen Mock Assembly)** for MVR and initial launch so any declared 40-item IELTS/VSTEP mock or 200-item TOEIC mock is 100% populated from day one using the 2,120-unit shared pool, and add **Option B (+416 locked mock units)** in Phase 2 if fixed cohort-wide mock forms are required by institutional clients.

| Option | Mechanism | Additional Items Required Beyond 2,120 Pool | Architectural Trade-Offs |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Option A (Dynamic Unseen Mock Assembly — Recommended for Launch)** | When a learner launches a full mock (e.g., 40-item IELTS/VSTEP Reading mock or 200-item TOEIC mock), the engine assembles a stratified blueprint dynamically from active pool items the learner has not yet seen (item\_id NOT IN seen\_item\_ids). | **0\\text{ extra items}** (Uses the 2,120 shared pool) | Zero additional authoring cost; guarantees every mock item is unseen by that learner. However, two learners taking "Mock Test 1" see different item sets, and a heavy TOEIC user taking three 200-item mocks consumes 300 L \+ 300 R items across bands. |
| **Option B (Dedicated Locked Mock Reserve — 2 Fixed Forms)** | Author 2 fixed, locked multi-level mock forms (placement\_eligible \= false, practice\_eligible \= false, mock\_form\_id \= 1..2) containing 200 L \+ 200 R items \+ 8 S \+ 8 W tasks spanning A2–C1. | **\+416\\text{ units}** (200\\text{ L} \+ 200\\text{ R} \+ 8\\text{ S} \+ 8\\text{ W}) | Provides fixed, standardized mock forms for cohort benchmarking and completely insulates topic practice stock from 200-item TOEIC mock depletion. Increases total pool from 2,120 to 2,536 units. |

### **8.3 Open Question 3: Domain Context Distribution (workplace vs. academic vs. general)**

Because one shared pool serves workplace exams (TOEIC L\&R, CAT, S\&W), academic/general exams (IELTS), and national multi-purpose exams (VSTEP), the proportion of stimuli authored in workplace, academic, and general contexts within each (topic\_id, cefr\_level) cell must be fixed before batch generation. **Recommendation:** Adopt **Option B (Band-Stratified Ecological Split)** so that A1–A2 cells avoid artificial academic lectures while B1–C2 cells provide balanced workplace and academic depth.

| Option | A1–A2 Domain Split | B1–B2 Domain Split | C1–C2 Domain Split | Validity & Authenticity Trade-Off |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Option A (Uniform Split)** | 33\\% Workplace / 33\\% Academic / 33\\% General | 33\\% Workplace / 33\\% Academic / 33\\% General | 33\\% Workplace / 33\\% Academic / 33\\% General | Simple arithmetic, but writing an academic journal or lecture stimulus at A1 or A2 violates CEFR descriptors, which restrict A1–A2 to routine personal and workplace transactions. |
| **Option B (Band-Stratified Ecological Split — Recommended)** | **50\\% General / 50\\% Workplace / 0\\% Academic** | **40\\% Workplace / 35\\% Academic / 25\\% General** | **45\\% Academic / 45\\% Workplace / 10\\% General** | Preserves natural linguistic authenticity at A1–A2 and C1–C2 while ensuring every independent and proficient band has rich workplace and academic coverage. |

### **8.4 Open Question 4: Audio Accent Proportions in the Listening Pool**

IELTS, TOEIC, and VSTEP expose test-takers to international English varieties. The TTS voice synthesis configuration must fix the accent distribution across the 330 Listening audio stimuli. **Recommendation:** Adopt **Option B (Balanced International Standard)** stratified evenly across every CEFR band (A1–C2) so learners preparing for any of the five certificates encounter authentic international phonology.

| Option | North American (en-US / en-CA) | British / Irish (en-GB / en-IE) | Australasian (en-AU) | Cross-Certificate Suitability |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Option A (North American Majority)** | 70\\% | 20\\% | 10\\% | Mirrors legacy TOEIC weighting but under-prepares IELTS and VSTEP candidates for British and Australian recordings. |
| **Option B (Balanced International Standard — Recommended)** | **45\\%** | **35\\%** | **20\\%** | Prepares learners simultaneously for TOEIC's four-accent standard and IELTS's Commonwealth/International balance. |
