# **Software Requirements Specification: Adaptive Learning Path Engine Rebuild**

## **1\. Operational Boundaries, Non-Objectives, and Surviving System Architecture**

### **1.1 Operational Mandate and System Context**

This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the complete functional, psychometric, algorithmic, and data-contractual architecture for rebuilding the learning path, practice orchestration, and instructional lesson subsystem of a multi-certificate English exam-practice platform. The specification governs the entire candidate lifecycle between initial diagnostic placement and formal examination sitting, encompassing plan synthesis, step composition, practice item selection, standalone free drilling, dual-axis progress telemetry, event-driven re-planning, and terminal readiness evaluation.  
This engineering initiative is an explicit delete-and-rebuild operation rather than an incremental refactoring effort. An empirical audit of the legacy production and seeded demonstration state on November 3, 2026 revealed severe structural defects that render the existing path, practice, and lesson machinery unsalvageable. The legacy planner enforced a hardcoded four-week horizon (WindowWeeks \= 4\) that terminated permanently at day 28 without generating subsequent schedules; when the final step closed, the path transitioned to completed and left candidates on multi-month preparation tracks facing an empty screen after week four. Furthermore, the legacy generator collapsed complex multi-skill learner proficiency into a single scalar starting band—reading placement, else the latest mock exam, else the goal—and pitched the entire study plan at that single level, even when a learner read at B2 and spoke at A2. It scheduled practice material by exam section (such as "practise section P5") without carrying any Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) level, rendering it incapable of consuming level-tagged items from the rebuilt placement pool. The legacy system also operated two mutually contradictory question selectors, relied on uncalibrated numerical heuristics—specifically a fixed 10-item practice session length and an arbitrary 75% mastery threshold—failed to measure whether mid-path step completion actually moved the candidate toward their target band, and lacked any practice-session model for Speaking and Writing. In the audited database state, 15 topic categories existed alongside 5 topic rows that no code read, yet only 3 topic categories contained any items at all (holding 2, 1, and 1 items respectively across a 116-item total bank distributed as 4, 62, 43, 7, and 0 items across difficulty levels 1 through 5). Simultaneously, 5 of 13 exam sections contained zero items, and 13 authored lessons lacked any level or band column while 12 of the 13 lacked topic associations.  
Meanwhile, empirical benchmarks from Cambridge Assessment English and the Association of Language Testers in Europe (ALTE) establish that advancing across CEFR bands requires substantial, cumulative guided learning hours—ranging from roughly 90–100 hours to reach A1 and 180–200 hours to reach A2, up to 350–400 hours for B1, 500–600 hours for B2, 700–800 hours for C1, and 1,000–1,200 hours for C2 (extending to 1,550 cumulative hours in comprehensive self-practice exam models). Bridging multi-hundred-hour proficiency gaps across multiple language modalities is structurally impossible within a static, un-leveled four-week checklist.  
The rebuilt engine supports five distinct certificate configurations that vary in both examined skills and native scoring scales. Because two of the five certificates examine only two skills while the remaining three examine four skills, all scheduling, capability estimation, and step lifecycle logic must remain strictly certificate-agnostic in its core state machine while isolating content and learner state by certificate partition.

| Certificate Configuration Code | Canonical Certificate Name | Examined Skills (Active Modalities) | Native Score / Band Scale | CEFR Reporting Ceiling & Track Characteristics |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **TOEIC\_LR** | TOEIC Listening & Reading (Comprehensive Track) | Listening, Reading, Speaking, Writing | L: 5–495; R: 5–495 (S/W auxiliary) | 4-skill comprehensive preparation track; native reporting caps at C1 |
| **IELTS** | IELTS Academic & General Training | Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking | Overall & Per-Skill: 0.0–9.0 (0.5 step) | 4-skill track spanning full A1–C2 continuum |

### **1.2 Architectural Invariants and Non-Objectives**

Five non-negotiable architectural invariants govern every subsystem specified in this document. First, practice sessions, learning path step execution, and placement tests are unconditionally free to the candidate; no transaction within the learning path or practice subsystem may ever generate a credit ledger deduction row. Second, strict cross-certificate isolation is enforced across all database queries and state projections so that one certificate's authored content, item exposure history, or path state never leaks into another certificate. Third, artificial intelligence models are restricted strictly to grading free-response productive tasks (Speaking and Writing) against analytical rubrics and generating human-readable diagnostic narrative; an AI model never chooses a path step, selects a practice item, computes a schedule, or assigns a deterministic mastery score. Fourth, derived values—including session accuracy, topic mastery probability, weekly completion percentage, and band distance to target—are computed deterministically from stored facts rather than stored redundantly across conflicting tables. Fifth, the architecture enforces one writer per domain fact, eliminating the historical defect in which separate writers disagreed about a single learner response.  
Adjacent domains are explicitly bounded out of this specification. Content pool volume ownership, item authoring validation pipelines, and analytical scoring rubrics belong entirely to the sibling *Unified Content Blueprint and Psychometric Authoring Specification*; Section 5 of this SRS states what the path engine requires from that pool and names explicit deficit gaps where the pool cannot supply it, rather than inventing a competing volume table. Similarly, placement test administration and internal Item Response Theory (IRT) Bayesian posterior estimations belong to the sibling placement specification, which hands off per-skill CEFR ratings and an overall rating via the immutable PlacementResultContract. Commercial pricing, subscription credit economics, client-side UI chrome animations, and v1 accessibility accommodations (cataloged for v2 in Section 6\) are also outside the core engine scope.

### **2.2 The Step Shape and Level Model (Answering §5.1 Q1, Q2 & §5.5 Q15)**

A PathStep never points at a certificate name or an un-leveled exam section (§5.1 Q1). Instead, every step points at a strongly typed pedagogical coordinate tuple defined as:  
\\text{StepCoordinate} \= (\\text{skill}, \\; \\text{topic\\\_id}, \\; \\text{target\\\_cefr}, \\; \\text{step\\\_type}, \\; \\text{sizing\\\_tier})  
Depending on step\_type, the step resolves its executable payload through one of three polymorphic bindings. Dynamic practice steps (diagnostic\_drill, focus\_practice, review\_spaced) point at (skill, topic\_id, target\_cefr) and store a deterministic ContentQueryDescriptor that resolves to prescribed\_item\_count items from the certificate's item pool at execution time. Instructional lesson steps (concept\_lesson, or an expanded\_remedial drill with an embedded lesson) point at (skill, topic\_id, target\_cefr) and bind directly to a leveled lesson\_id (module\_primary for initial instruction, or module\_primary followed by module\_remedial for low-mastery remediation) paired with guided check items. Formal sitting steps (mock\_section) point at (skill, target\_cefr) and delegate execution to the surviving ExamAttemptEngine (§4.3) using a certificate-specific exam\_section\_id to recalibrate the candidate's per-skill CEFR level. Because skill is drawn dynamically from the active modalities of the candidate's chosen certificate (M\_activ\[span\_133\](start\_span)\[span\_133\](end\_span)e \= {L, R} for TOEIC\_CAT, {S, W} for TOEIC\_SW, and {L, R, S, W} for TOEIC\_LR, IELTS, and VSTEP), the identical step schema and scheduling engine operate across two-skill and four-skill certificates without any certificate-named conditional logic.  
To resolve how a step carries a level when legacy lessons lacked a level column (§5.1 Q2), the 13 un-leveled legacy lesson rows are deleted (§1.3), and the Lessons entity is rebuilt with mandatory skill, topic\_id, and cefr\_level (A1–C2) attributes. Attaching an un-leveled lesson to a leveled step would force the system to serve advanced C1 academic discourse framing to an A2 learner or elementary A1 scaffolding to a B2 learner, undermining diagnostic precision. In the rebuilt schema, each (skill, topic\_id, cefr\_level) cell in the 20-topic by 6-level matrix maps to a canonical lesson containing two structured sub-modules: module\_primary (core strategy framing and worked examples) and module\_remedial (simplified scaffolding and contrastive error analysis for candidates who fail a mastery check).  
To resolve how a step's level is chosen when placement returns four distinct skill levels alongside an overall level (§5.5 Q15), the engine implements **Decoupled Per-Skill Level Selection**. The sibling placement engine emits a PlacementResultContract containing four independent per-skill CEFR ratings (\\text{CEFR}\_L, \\text{CEFR}\_R, \\text{CEFR}\_S, \\text{CEFR}\_W), intra-band positions (P\_s \\in \[0, 100\]), continuous ability coordinates (\\theta\_s \= k\_s \+ P\_s / 100 \\in \[1.00, 6.99\] where integer band indices map \\text{A1}=1 \\dots \\text{C2}=6), and an aggregated overall\_cefr. While P\[span\_205\](start\_span)\[span\_205\](end\_span)\[span\_250\](start\_span)\[span\_250\](end\_span)3 establishes that the candidate follows one integrated timeline with steps assigned to individual skills, the difficulty level target\_cefr (L\_{\\text{step}}) of any step assigned to skill s is anchored directly to that specific skill's current CEFR rating (L\_{\\text{step}} \= \\text{CEFR}\_s), never to overall\_cefr. For example, if a candidate places at \\text{Listening} \= \\text{B2}, \\text{Reading} \= \\text{B2}, \\text{Writing} \= \\text{B1}, and \\text{Speaking} \= \\text{A2} (with overall\_cefr \= B1), Listening and Reading steps are instantiated at B2, Writing steps at B1, and Speaking steps at A2. Within skill s and topic j, target\_cefr remains at \\text{CEFR}\_s (on\_level) while LearnerTopicCapability(s, j, CEFR\_s).mastery\_band \!= 'mastered'. Once the candidate achieves mastered status in topic j at \\text{CEFR}\_s, holds an intra-band position P\_s \\ge 75, and targets a certificate band above \\text{CEFR}\_s, subsequent steps in topic j advance to \\text{CEFR}\_s \+ 1 (stretch\_level) to scaffold progression into the next band. Under no circumstances does a high overall\_cefr pull a lagging skill's step level upward, nor does a low overall\_cefr depress an advanced skill's step level downward.

### **2.3 The Unified Topic Taxonomy (Answering §5.3 Q9)**

A mastery model over topics is only as valid as the granularity and population of its topic taxonomy; with only 3 of 15 legacy topics populated, "weakest topic" routing was previously meaningless (§2.8, §5.3 Q9). In the rebuilt architecture, a Topic belongs to the English language and human communicative cognition rather than to a specific testing brand, meaning morphosyntactic tense control (R-GRAM) or pragmatic implication (L-INF) represents the same underlying construct across TOEIC, IELTS, and VSTEP. However, because each certificate enforces strict item-format conventions and register distributions, authored items are partitioned by certificate\_code (§1.2), and a candidate's LearnerTopicCapability rows are scoped per (user\_id, certificate\_code) (P8) so switching certificates switches paths cleanly. Every canonical topic is crossed orthogonally with all six CEFR levels (A1 through C2), yielding 20 \\times 6 \= 120 capability cells per certificate.  
Furthermore, the grain of a topic is calibrated to the cognitive structure of each modality (§5.3 Q9). Receptive skills (Listening and Reading) decompose into 6 micro-comprehension and linguistic topics each (12 objective topics), because discrete multiple-choice items isolate specific decoding operations. Productive skills (Speaking and Writing) decompose into 4 communicative task-genre topics each (8 productive topics), because spoken monologues and written essays integrate multiple linguistic dimensions simultaneously; productive topics are therefore coupled with a 4-criterion analytical rubric profile (c1 through c4) in LearnerSkillState.criterion\_profile that diagnoses sub-skill weaknesses across tasks.

## **3\. Functional Requirements**

All functional requirements are numbered, deterministic, and testable, organized across the eight functional domains required by Section 7.3 of the brief.

### **3.1 Generating a Path**

The path generation subsystem initializes the candidate's multi-skill state from placement, evaluates schedule feasibility via the surviving calculator, and maintains the continuous four-week rolling horizon (P1, P8). Rather than generating a static multi-month block that drifts out of alignment by week six or stops dead after day 28, the state machine maintains three distinct temporal zones: an immutable historical archive of completed weeks (window\_slot \= NULL), an active four-week sliding window (window\_slot \= 1, 2, 3, 4 representing W\_1, W\_2, W\_3, W\_4), and a capability-driven window synthesizer that generates Week N+4 into slot W\_4 whenever W\_1 completes.

### **3.6 Event-Driven Re-Planning (Answering §3.1 P5 & §5.5 Q16, Q17)**

## **4\. Business Rules and Operational Invariants**

### **4.1 Reconciliation of Capability Reading (L5) and Completion Closure (P4) (Answering §5.4 Q14)**

A critical domain interaction arises between L5 (*"the path reads capability, not activity logs—everything the learner does counts toward mastery, including free drilling"*) and P4 (*"a step closes by doing it, not by improving"*) (§5.4 Q14). Business Rule **BR-RECON-01** reconciles P\[span\_156\](start\_span)\[span\_156\](end\_span)4, L5, L6, and L7 without contradiction: **free drilling never closes a scheduled calendar step, but directly shrinks the size of downstream steps via capability updates**. Specifically, a PathStep row in PathWeeks is a scheduled activity on the candidate's calendar that transitions to closed exclusively when executed through StepClosureWriter (P4). Moreover, because L6 keeps free drilling locked until all steps in the active week W\_1 are closed, a candidate cannot enter free drilling while W\_1 still has open steps. Once W\_1's steps are completed and free drilling unlocks for the remainder of the week, every free drill session immediately updates LearnerTopicCapability.mastery\_probability and mastery\_band (L5). When the sliding window advances at the end of W\_1 (or when buffered steps in W\_2 \\dots W\_4 evaluate their dynamic sizing tier upon unlocking), the planner reads LearnerTopicCapability (L5): any topic elevated to mastery\_band \= 'mastered' during free drilling automatically resizes its scheduled steps in W\_2 \\dots W\_4 from standard (10 items) or expanded\_remedial (lesson \+ 15 items) down to short\_maintenance (5 items) (L7). Thus, free drilling cannot be used to skip the plan, but rewards the candidate by making subsequent weeks faster and lighter.

### **4.2 Conflict Resolution: Path Step Versus Item Cooldown (Answering §5.4 Q13)**

In the legacy system, standalone practice enforced a 24-hour cooldown after a wrong answer, whereas path practice ignored cooldowns and could re-serve the exact item missed minutes earlier (§2.5). Business Rule **BR-COOL-01** governs what happens when a scheduled PathStep requires topic T, but the candidate recently missed items in topic T (§5.4 Q13): **the path step remains executable on the calendar, and the cooldown wins at the individual item level**. Whenever a candidate answers an objective item i incorrectly, LearnerItemExposure.cooldown\_until is set to \\text{NOW()} \+ 24\\text{ hours}. Whenever a candidate answers an objective item i correctly, cooldown\_until is set to \\text{NOW()} \+ 72\\text{ hours} for discrete R-GRAM items or \\text{NOW()} \+ 14\\text{ days} for passage/audio comprehension items (R-GIST..R-ATT, L-GIST..L-ORG). A scheduled PathStep is never locked or blocked because items in its topic are cooling down (P6: never schedule a step that cannot be completed). Instead, when the candidate opens the step, the unified selector (FR-MAST-01) excludes all cooling-down item\_ids and serves unseen or uncooled items from the same (topic\_id, target\_cefr) cell. If every single item in that (topic\_id, target\_cefr) cell is currently inside the 24-hour wrong-answer cooldown window, the selector invokes the **Substitution Cascade (P6, BR-POOL-01)**—filling the step with unseen items from the closest sibling topic in the same (skill, target\_cefr) and rendering a disclosure banner explaining that related sibling-topic items have been substituted while recently missed questions cool down (or, only if every topic in the entire skill band is simultaneously in 24-hour cooldown, lifting the cooldown on the oldest-exposed item in the cell ordered by last\_seen\_at ASC).

## **7\. Empirical Foundations in Cognitive Psychology, Psychometrics, and Second Language Acquisition**

To enable the implementing engineering team to verify every algorithmic rule against peer-reviewed literature (§7.7), this section synthesizes the cognitive psychology, psychometric, and Second Language Acquisition (SLA) research governing the learning path engine, including the explicit domain transfer boundaries of spaced repetition.

### **7.1 Spaced Repetition: Where It Transfers to Grammar and Reading Comprehension and Where It Fails**

Commercial language platforms frequently commit a category error by applying paired-associate declarative memory spacing algorithms—such as Ebbinghaus exponential forgetting curves (Ebbinghaus, 1885), SuperMemo SM-2 (Wozniak, 1990), or Duolingo's Half-Life Regression (Settles & Meeder, 2016)—indiscriminately across all language modalities. Settles and Meeder (2016) formulated Half-Life Regression (HLR) to predict recall probability p \= 2^{-\\Delta / h} with estimated memory half-life h \= 2^{\\vec{\\theta} \\cdot \\vec{x}} specifically over discrete lexical translations and morphological inflections. Meta-analytic SLA research by Kim and Webb (2022) (*Language Learning*) and empirical studies by Suzuki, Yokosawa, and Aline (2020) confirm that temporal spacing yields medium-to-large positive effects on L2 retention compared to massed practice, with longer spacing intervals outperforming shorter intervals on delayed posttests. However, cognitive psychology and Skill Acquisition Theory (DeKeyser, 1997, 2007\) establish a strict boundary between **discrete morphosyntactic rule retrieval** and **discourse-level reading/listening comprehension and productive composition**.  
Spaced repetition transfers directly to **explicit morphosyntactic rules, sentence-level error identification, discourse signposts, and fixed lexical collocations (R-GRAM, R-COH, L-ORG)**. Learning verb tense-aspect contrasts, conditional subordination, agreement morphology, and prepositional collocations involves declarative rule encodings that gradually proceduralize through distributed retrieval episodes (DeKeyser, 1997, 2007; Suzuki et al., 2020). Empirical studies by Pan, Tajran, Lovelett, Osuna, and Rickard (2019) (*Journal of Educational Psychology*) and Nakata and Suzuki (2019) (*The Modern Language Journal*) demonstrate that spacing and interleaving L2 grammar exercises across multi-day intervals significantly improves delayed grammaticality judgment and morphosyntactic accuracy because each spaced trial forces active reconstruction of the form-function mapping from long-term memory rather than relying on transient working-memory echoes. Consequently, the engine applies Half-Life Regression stability tracking (FR-MAST-04, BR-DECAY-01) specifically to R-GRAM and discrete structural signposts.  
Conversely, item-level spaced repetition **fails completely for reading comprehension (R-GIST, R-DET, R-INF, R-ATT), listening comprehension (L-GIST..L-FUNC), and productive speaking and writing tasks (S-\*, W-\*)**. Under Kintsch's (1988, 1998\) Construction-Integration model of text comprehension, answering an inferential or main-idea question on a 450-word reading passage or a 90-second audio dialogue requires real-time lexical decoding, syntactic parsing, and the construction of an episodic situation model integrating textual premises. If an engine re-serves the exact same reading passage or audio track at 3-, 7-, and 14-day intervals, the candidate retrieves their **episodic memory of the passage content and the remembered answer key** rather than executing online reading or listening comprehension. Re-administering comprehension stimuli measures passage recall rather than comprehension skill, violates local item independence, and inflates capability estimates without transferring to unseen texts. Similarly, in Speaking and Writing (S-\*, W-\*), repeating an identical prompt on an expanding schedule promotes memorized script recitation rather than spontaneous rhetorical planning, lexical retrieval, and acoustic articulation (DeKeyser, 2007; Suzuki, 2021). Therefore, FR-MAST-04 strictly prohibits item-level repetition in comprehension and productive topics, enforcing the novelty lock (exclude\_item\_ids) so that spaced review schedules **cognitive topic retrieval over novel unseen stimuli**.

### **7.2 Blocked-to-Interleaved Sequencing ("Increasing Practice") and Skill Acquisition**

The intra-skill and cross-skill sequencing rules in FR-SCHED-03 (§5.2 Q6) are grounded in the **Desirable Difficulty Framework** (Bjork, 1994; Suzuki, Nakata, & DeKeyser, 2019\) and the **Discriminative Contrast Hypothesis** (Kang & Pashler, 2012; Taylor & Rohrer, 2010). In four experiments on L2 verb conjugation, Pan et al. (2019) demonstrated that interleaved practice across contrasting tenses yielded superior delayed retention compared to blocked practice. Likewise, Nakata and Suzuki (2019) tested blocked, interleaved, and increasing practice across five English grammatical categories (simple past, present perfect, and first, second, and third conditionals), finding that although interleaving produced more errors during training, it significantly outperformed blocking on delayed grammaticality posttests by forcing learners to notice discriminative contextual cues rather than applying a single rule mechanically.  
Crucially, however, prior knowledge moderates the interleaving effect: Nakata and Suzuki (2019), Hwang (2025) (*Language Learning*, demonstrating that premature interleaving creates undesirable difficulty for low-proficiency learners before declarative knowledge is established), and Suzuki (2021) (*Studies in Second Language Acquisition*, showing that consecutive blocked practice promotes initial L2 oral fluency proceduralization whereas interleaved practice promotes pragmatic accuracy) establish that beginners benefit from initial blocked exposure before transitioning to mixed practice. Porter et al. and Nakata and Suzuki (2019) formalize this hybrid progression as **Increasing Practice**—presenting blocked instruction and single-category practice first to establish declarative form-meaning mappings and initial proceduralization, followed by interleaved multi-category practice that increases contextual interference as proficiency grows. The weekly scheduler in FR-SCHED-03 directly implements Increasing Practice by sequencing concept\_lesson \\to blocked single-topic diagnostic\_drill / focus\_practice \\to interleaved review\_spaced / mock\_section.
