# Research brief — Admin and authoring interface

**Audience:** one external deep-research agent. **No repository access** — self-contained.

**What you deliver:** an SRS for the staff-facing interface: the tools that put content into the pool, review it, and keep it correct. Plus the rules that decide who may do what.

**Scope:** admin and authoring screens only. The learner interface has its own brief. Pricing is out.

**Why this exists:** three sibling briefs rebuild the placement test, the learning path, and the learner
interface. All three assume content exists. Someone has to make it exist, check it, and retire it — and
today that tooling is partial, scattered, and in places dangerous.

---

## 1. The product

A multi-certificate English exam-practice application. Learners take a placement test, follow a learning
path, drill, and sit mock exams. Content is items (questions), tasks (free-response prompts), lessons,
exams, and certificates.

Five certificates, and **they do not all have four skills:**

| Certificate | Skills examined |
|---|---|
| TOEIC Listening & Reading | Listening, Reading, Speaking, Writing |
| IELTS Academic & General | Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking |
| VSTEP (Vietnam national test) | Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking |
| TOEIC Multi-Stage Adaptive (CAT) | Listening, Reading |
| TOEIC Speaking & Writing | Speaking, Writing |

**Domain vocabulary:** Certificate, **Skill**, **Section** (a part of a paper), **Topic** (a sub-area within
a skill), **Exam**, **Item**, **Task** (a free-response prompt), **Lesson** (cards), **Practice session**,
**Step** (an activity in a path), **Free response**, **Band**, **CEFR level**, **Pool** (the shared item
store feeding every certificate).

**The pool is shared.** An item is not authored "for TOEIC" — it is authored at a CEFR level for a skill,
and any certificate whose skills include that skill may use it. This shapes the authoring tool more than
anything else: you are not writing an exam, you are stocking a warehouse that several exams draw from.

## 2. What the staff surface does today

Ten admin controllers and 31 views: certificates, exams, lessons, mentors, orders, packages, users,
complaints.

Three things are known to be weak, and they are the reason this brief exists:

### 2.1 The exam studio was rebuilt once and is still heavy

It was reduced from three panes to two, and a four-option multiple-choice item from roughly eighteen
visible inputs to fourteen. That reduction was driven by a specific complaint: authoring 200 questions
was painful enough that nobody finished. It is better than it was, not good.

### 2.2 Several authoring routes are blocked on purpose

Publishing is one door now — a single publish action after an audit — because three ways round it had
been found: a status dropdown that published a zero-question exam in one form post, a blank expected
count that passed an empty section, and rewriting a question on an exam learners had already sat. A
structurally locked exam refuses edits that could change a recorded score.

That gate is correct and must survive any rebuild. It is also, today, expressed as errors a user meets
after the fact rather than as a workflow that prevents them.

### 2.3 Content arrived without a specification, and the tooling could not tell

Measured from the demo database: 116 items across five certificates and four skills; **zero items at the
hardest difficulty level**; **twelve of fifteen topic categories with no items at all**; eight sections
declaring thirty or more items with single digits in them; five sections with **no items**; an IELTS mock
declaring 40 items and holding 2.

An authoring tool that had enforced a specification would have made most of those impossible. It did not,
because there was no specification to enforce. A sibling brief is producing one — this brief is about the
tool that must apply it.

### 2.4 Authoring is not one job

The person who writes items, the person who reviews them, and the person who decides a certificate's
structure are treated as one role today. That is why review is informal and why the audit gate is
experienced as an obstacle.

## 3. Decisions already taken

Settled by the sibling briefs and by earlier work. Build on them.

| # | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Content is generated by AI, validated automatically, then a 10% sample is human-reviewed.** No pilot study. | Reviewing every item is not realistic; the risk of an unmeasured label error is accepted. |
| A2 | **The pool is uploaded as JSON, one file per skill**, validated with per-line errors. | Bulk load is the only practical way to fill hundreds of items. |
| A3 | **Publishing is one door** — a single action after an audit, never a status field. | Three bypasses existed; each one shipped something nobody reviewed. |
| A4 | **An exam with finished attempts locks its structure**: no adding, removing or moving items, no answer-key edits, no question-type change. Cosmetic fields stay editable. | A structural edit can change a score that was already recorded. |
| A5 | **Items are shared across certificates**, authored at a CEFR level for a skill. | One pool serves five certificates; two of them examine only two skills. |
| A6 | **The demo set is marked in the data, not implemented as separate code.** | Two code paths drift apart. |

## 4. What the SRS must answer

### 4.1 The two populations

1. **Who uses this interface, and are they the same person?** An item author, a reviewer, a certificate
   designer, and someone handling complaints are four jobs. Today they are one role. Define the roles and
   what each may do.
2. **What is the review workflow** when a machine has already validated and a human samples 10%? Who
   approves, what do they see, and what happens to the 90% nobody looked at?
3. **What does an author see when their item is rejected**, and can they fix it without re-uploading
   everything?

### 4.2 Bulk authoring

4. **What does the upload look like end to end** — file, validation, per-line errors, preview, commit,
   rollback? A file of 300 items where item 247 is wrong must not lose the 246 before it.
5. **How does an author see what a generated item will look like to a learner** before it enters the pool?
6. **What does the tool show about coverage** — how many items exist per skill per level per topic, and
   where the gaps are? This is the view that would have prevented twelve empty topics.
7. **Can an author edit an item that is already in the pool**, and what happens to learners' history that
   references it?

### 4.3 Item authoring, one at a time

8. **What are the minimum controls for one item?** The exam studio was reduced to fourteen controls for a
   four-option MCQ and that is still considered heavy. What does an item actually need?
9. **How are the two hard shapes authored** — one audio serving several items, one passage serving several
   items? These are the shapes that decide whether the format is usable, and the ones a form usually
   handles badly.
10. **How is audio attached and trimmed?** Today a path text box and hand-typed millisecond bounds were
    replaced by a file picker and two player buttons. Say whether that is the right model and what is
    still missing.
11. **What does the level control look like** when level is CEFR and must be defensible? A dropdown of six
    values is easy to add and easy to get wrong.

### 4.4 Rubrics

12. **How does an administrator write band descriptors** for a rubric criterion? Today most criteria have
    a name and a maximum score and no descriptor at any level, which means the AI has been grading
    against a scale nobody wrote down. This is the single most valuable authoring screen in the product
    and it does not exist.
13. **How does an administrator see whether a rubric discriminates** — that it separates adjacent levels
    rather than compressing everything into the middle?

### 4.5 Certificates and exams

14. **What is the certificate blueprint workflow?** A certificate has skills, sections per skill, a band
    table, a scoring rule, and section-level expectations. Today a wizard collects this in steps. Is that
    the right shape, and where does it break when a certificate has two skills instead of four?
15. **How does the audit gate become guidance rather than a wall?** The gate must survive (A3, A4). The
    question is whether the interface can show why an exam is not publishable *while* it is being edited,
    instead of only when the publish button is pressed.
16. **What can an administrator do about an exam that is already locked** because learners have sat it?
    Clone it? Version it? Nothing?

### 4.6 The dangerous operations

17. **What is the confirmation model for destructive actions** — deleting a section with 40 items,
    retiring a topic, deactivating a certificate that learners are currently studying?
18. **What is soft versus hard deletion** for each content type? A learner's history may reference an
    item; deleting the item must not silently break their record.
19. **What does an administrator see about the consequence** of a change before making it — "six learners
    are currently on a path that uses this"?

## 5. Deliverable format

An SRS with:

1. Scope and non-goals.
2. **Role model** — who may do what, stated as permissions rather than job titles.
3. **Screen inventory** — every staff-facing screen, its purpose, and its states.
4. **Per-screen specification**: layout in words, the controls, validation, and what it prevents.
5. **Workflows** authored as sequences: upload and review, publish, rubric authoring, item retirement,
   the locked-exam case.
6. **The coverage view** — what it must show so that empty topics and thin sections are visible before
   they ship.
7. **Danger rules** — confirmation, soft versus hard deletion, what happens to learner history.
8. **Open questions** with options and your recommendation.
9. **References** to real sources on content operations, review workflows, and assessment item banking.

---

## 6. Known gaps, deliberately not solved here

- **Pricing, packages, orders, wallet.** Out of scope; a separate concern.
- **Accessibility.** Out of scope for v1 by the owner's decision.
- **Mobile.** Desktop-first at 1440×900 by recorded choice.
- **Mentors and complaints.** These exist and work; they are not part of the content rebuild.

---

## 7. What to be careful about

- **The publish gate and the structural lock are not obstacles to remove.** They exist because three
  bypasses shipped unreviewed content and because a structural edit can rewrite a recorded score. Making
  the workflow smoother is the goal; making the gate optional is not.
- **This is a warehouse, not an exam editor.** Items are shared across five certificates. Any screen that
  assumes "this item belongs to this exam" is wrong.
- **Do not design for one certificate.** Two of five examine only two skills.
- **The 14-control target is a ceiling, not a benchmark.** The studio was reduced from eighteen and is
  still considered heavy. Do not add controls back without saying what is removed.
- **Rubric descriptors are content, not configuration.** Most are missing. Any design that treats them as
  optional metadata will leave the AI grading against nothing.
- **A deleted item can break a learner's history.** Retirement, not deletion, is the safer default — say
  which you chose and why.
- **Do not invent the content specification.** How many items each level needs is fixed by a sibling
  brief. Your job is the tool that applies it.
