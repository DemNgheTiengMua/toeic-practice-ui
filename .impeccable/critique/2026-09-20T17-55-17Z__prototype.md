## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored for this product in structure and vocabulary; category-interchangeable in appearance.**

Three things could not be transplanted to a generic exam-prep app. The copy is Vietnamese-first and *operational*: "Bắt đầu thi — dùng 1 lượt", "Huỷ là mất lượt thi đã trừ", "Đối chiếu khuôn mặt trên ảnh chân dung với ảnh trên CCCD", "Dạng bẫy hay gặp ở Part 5: đưa V-ing vào vị trí động từ chính". The domain's real asymmetries are *encoded*: the CEFR track is drawn as a proportional six-band scale using the actual 10–990 thresholds (`grid-template-columns: 110fr 105fr 325fr 235fr 160fr 45fr`), Listening blocks backward part navigation, audio is declared non-seekable, and credit deduction is ordered strictly after KYC. And the abnormal branches are named in the product's own vocabulary: `exam-list.html:11` carries eight states, five of them failure or gate conditions.

The counter-evidence is the surface. The card system, `.stat` tiles, `.price-card` trio, `.badge` palette and the `#1f6feb`-on-`#f6f7f9` scheme are a competent generic admin-dashboard kit. Nothing about the *look* says TOEIC, or Vietnam, or "I need 650 to graduate." Swap the Vietnamese strings for English and the skin would serve a project-management tool unchanged.

**The 385 collapse to roughly 6–8 root causes.** 315 `low-contrast` findings reduce to **7 distinct colour pairs / 6 tokens** in `tokens.css`. 55 `side-tab` findings reduce to **2 rules** (`components.css:199` `.alert`, `:451` `.explain`). `index.html` alone reports 146 findings — **143 of them the byte-identical string** `:hover 4.3:1 — #1f6feb on #f6f7f9`, because the catalog has one anchor per state of every page; the detector counts elements, not rules. That one artifact is 37% of the total. Line numbers are `0` for every HTML finding, so file attribution is usable but line attribution is not.

### Where the two assessments meet

**They agree on contrast.** The LLM review independently measured `.text-sm.text-muted` at 4.42:1, `.cefr__band` at 4.19:1, `.badge--success` at 4.43:1 and `--color-warning` at 3.40:1 — all below the 4.5:1 AA threshold for normal text, and all inside the token set the detector flagged. Two methods, same six tokens.

**They agree on line length, from opposite directions.** The LLM review measured `.cefr__note` at ~174 chars/line and flagged it as a reading problem on the screen where a learner actually reads. The browser overlay has a `line-length` rule the **CLI never emits at all** — a genuine gap between the two tools.

**One productive disagreement.** The LLM review praised `.explain` as the product's best moment — the only screen that treats a wrong answer as a lesson. The detector flags the same element's 4px left accent bar (`components.css:451`) as a slop tell. Both are right about different properties: the *content* is the strongest thing in the prototype; the *decoration* is a generic pattern. Worth separating rather than resolving.

### Where the detector caught what the review missed

The review noticed the red "Nộp bài sớm" button in the exam sidebar — and misdiagnosed it as an *attention* problem (constant low-grade pressure). The detector found the real defect: `.btn--danger` has **no `:hover` override**, so it inherits `.btn:hover { background: var(--color-bg) }` from `components.css:14` while keeping `color: #ffffff`. That is **white on `#f6f7f9` — 1.1:1, invisible.** Verified with computed styles at rest (`rgb(255,255,255)` on `rgb(198,40,40)`) versus hover (`rgb(255,255,255)` on `rgb(246,247,249)`). `.btn--primary` escapes only because it has an override at line 23. This is a defect no amount of visual review would reliably surface, because it exists only in a hover state.

### Where the review caught what the detector missed

The detector's rule set has no behavioural rules. The review found, by driving the live page, that `checkout.html`'s payment selector **does not respond to clicks** — a functional bug invisible to a static scan. It also found the missing live regions, the missing headings, the absent responsive breakpoints, and the modal's broken focus containment. None of these are expressible as a detector rule.

### False positives

One. The lone `nested-cards` hit (`admin/kyc-review.html`) is a `.card` inside a `.modal` — the div tree contains no card-in-card, and a dialog is not a card. Correctly identified as a false positive and not carried into the priority list. Separately, `side-tab` is reported against 27 HTML files when the true source is 2 stylesheet rules; and whether a 4px alert accent bar constitutes an AI-generation tell is a design judgment, not a mechanical fact.

### Visual overlays

Injection was live, not fallback: mutation preflight (setting `document.title` and appending a `<script>`) passed on all 9 page-loads before `detect.js` was injected, and `typeof window.impeccableScan === "function"` confirmed the overlay loaded. The detector logs under the `impeccable` console namespace. Console findings totalled **34 across 9 page-loads**.

**State variants change the numbers enormously, and this is a measurement trap.** The default state of a page is its *first* declared state, which is `loading` on both `dashboard.html` and `exam-result.html`. So `exam-result.html` scores **1 finding at its default state and 9 at `success`**; `practice-take.html` goes **2 → 6**. A naive single-load audit under-reports those by 5× and 9×.

One tooling note with product implications: `state-switch.js` **silently falls back to `states[0]`** for an unrecognised `?state=` value. `?state=fail` on `exam-result.html` (which declares `loading,error,success`) renders `loading` without complaint. A typo'd or stale deep link therefore renders a plausible-looking wrong state rather than failing loudly.

## Overall Impression

This is a prototype that did the expensive work first and the cheap work last, which is the right order for its stated job — locking business flow before an ASP.NET Core build starts — but leaves the surface visibly unfinished. The best content in the repository is not a screen at all: it is the recorded *reasoning* in the markup (`"Gate này đứng TRƯỚC paywall và trước khi trừ credit"`, `"Chặn sau nút đó là đã trừ lượt rồi mới báo thiếu điều kiện"`), which turns a mockup into a testable specification and makes the paywall-before-deduction bug un-introducible by a developer who reads the file. Against that, the visual skin is a generic admin kit, two P0 operability bugs sit on the two surfaces that matter most, and the identity-verification moment — the deepest emotional valley in the product — has no reassurance in it at all.

**Single biggest opportunity:** the state machine is the prototype's best asset and it is invisible to the learner. It exists for the reviewer via the switcher. Surfacing "where am I, and what conditions do I still need to meet" as a persistent learner-facing element would convert three separate walls (KYC, credits, readiness) into one progressing state — and would fix the working-memory failure, the discovery-order problem, and the payment-before-disclosure problem together.

## What's Working

**1. Abnormal branches are first-class citizens, and the reasoning is recorded in the markup.**
`exam-list.html:11` declares eight states including `kyc-required`, `kyc-pending`, `paywall` and `cancelled`; `exam-listening.html:11` declares `resumed`, `expired`, `submitting`, `offline`. Critically the *why* is written down — `exam-list.html:114-117` and `exam-instructions.html:38-39` both state the gate must precede the credit-consuming button. For a prototype whose job is to lock flow before real code, this is the highest-value content in the repository: a testable specification, not a mockup.

**2. The admin KYC rejection path validates in place and coaches the message.**
`admin/kyc-review.html:246-290` — clicking "Từ chối" with an empty reason does not navigate. It unhides `#kyc-reason-error` with an actionable message, adds `field--invalid`, and moves focus to the textarea. Paired with the hint at `:229` telling the admin not to write "ảnh không hợp lệ" because the learner sees the text verbatim, this is the only place in the prototype where a form treats its error message as a product decision rather than a validation artifact.

**3. The result screen closes the learning loop instead of reporting a number.**
`exam-result.html` sequences score hero with delta and section splits → CEFR track with reached/current bands and a complete `role="img"` label → honest conversion disclaimer → per-part table whose arithmetic checks out (82+74=156; 200−156=44; 19 blank + 25 wrong = 44) → strengths vs. improvements. Then `.explain` tells the learner *why* they got it wrong. The product understands a TOEIC score is only useful if it produces a next action.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Answer selection is not operable
**What:** `student/practice-take.html:67` declares `<div class="option-list" role="radiogroup" aria-label="Đáp án câu 7">` containing four bare `<label class="option">` elements with a marker and text span — **and no `<input>` of any kind** (verified: 0 inputs in the block). A screen reader announces a radiogroup with zero radios; a keyboard user has nothing to focus. On the exam surfaces the markup differs but is no better: `exam-listening.html:126-134` has a real radio, but it is clipped to 1×1px (`components.css:124`) and the visible selection is driven by a **hardcoded** `option--selected` class that never moves. The intended feedback — `.option:has(.option__input:checked)` at `components.css:108-113` — never fires on any student surface, because `option__input` exists only in `admin/question-editor.html` (verified: 7 occurrences, one file).

**Why it matters:** A learner cannot answer a question without a mouse; on the free-practice surface they cannot answer at all. This is a hard stop, not a degradation, and it lands on the product's flagship free surface.
**Fix:** Pick one option pattern and use it everywhere. Give every `.option` a real `<input type="radio" class="option__input">` as first child, delete every hardcoded `option--selected`, and let `:has(.option__input:checked)` plus `:has(.option__input:focus-visible)` drive all visual state. Verify or remove the `@supports not (selector(:has(*)))` fallback, which currently guards nothing.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P0] The payment method selector does not respond to clicks
**What:** `payment/checkout.html:78-96` renders two real radios (verified live: `w:13, h:13, appearance:"auto", opacity:"1"` — visible native controls beside the custom numbered markers) inside `role="radiogroup"`. The radios carry **no `option__input` class** (verified in source: `name="method"` only), so the `:has(.option__input:checked)` rule does not match and the only selection cue is a hardcoded `option--selected` on the VNPay label. Live test: after clicking MoMo, `checkedIndex` became `1` while computed styles were **unchanged** — option 1 kept `border=rgb(31,111,235) bg=rgb(238,244,254)`, option 2 stayed `border=rgb(227,230,234) bg=rgb(255,255,255)`. `CSS.supports('selector(:has(*))')` is `true`, so this is purely the class-name mismatch.

**Why it matters:** The learner pays with a method they did not choose. Three signals disagree — VNPay highlighted, MoMo not, a native dot next to MoMo — at the highest-anxiety moment in the flow.
**Fix:** Add `class="option__input"` to both radios, delete `option--selected` from the markup, confirm `:has()` takes over. Then remove the redundant numbered markers; with a real radio present they are noise.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] `.btn--danger` hover renders its label invisible at 1.1:1
**What:** `components.css` defines `.btn:hover { background: var(--color-bg) }` at line 14, overrides for `.btn--primary:hover` (23) and `.btn--ghost:hover` (25) — and **no `.btn--danger:hover`** (declaration at 26). So a danger button keeps `color: #ffffff` while inheriting the light-gray hover background. Verified on the live "Nộp bài luôn" button in `exam-confirm-submit.html`'s submit dialog: at rest `rgb(255,255,255)` on `rgb(198,40,40)`; on hover `rgb(255,255,255)` on `rgb(246,247,249)`. **Five pages affected:** `kyc-review`, `gateway-mock`, `exam-confirm-submit`, `exam-list`, `exam-listening`.

**Why it matters:** This is the button that confirms you are finished with a paid exam. The text vanishing on hover reads as a broken page at the moment the learner is committing an irreplaceable credit. It also exists only in a hover state, so visual review alone will not reliably catch it.
**Fix:** Add `.btn--danger:hover { background: var(--color-danger-hover); }` alongside the existing variants. One rule.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P1] The payment surfaces contradict the product record
**What:** Two distinct contradictions. `payment/pricing.html:107` and `:119` sell "Thống kê điểm mạnh yếu theo part" as a paid differentiator on the 199.000 ₫ and 349.000 ₫ tiers, while `PRODUCT.md` states the diagnostic loop is free and stays free. Separately, a grep for CCCD/KYC/xác thực across all five `payment/*.html` files returns **no matches** — a learner can pay 349.000 ₫ and only then discover the real exam is gated behind identity verification.

**Why it matters:** A learner who already used the free weakness analysis reasonably concludes it was a trial. And the gate that will block their purchase is disclosed nowhere on the path to purchase.
**Fix:** Remove "Thống kê điểm mạnh yếu theo part" from the paid tiers and replace it with something genuinely gated, or label it as included free on every tier. Add one line near the pricing heading: "Thi thật cần xác thực CCCD một lần — miễn phí, không trừ lượt."
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The UI assumes one kind of user: sighted, desktop, mouse
**What:** Three independent assumptions. (a) `aria-live` appears **0 times prototype-wide**, so `.save-status` (changing from "Đã lưu" to "Mất kết nối — đang thử lại…") and the countdown timer — the only two elements that change without navigation — are silent to assistive technology. (b) `exam-listening.html` and `practice-take.html` contain **zero** headings: a 120-minute exam with nothing announcing where you are. (c) `grep "@media"` returns exactly one match prototype-wide (`components.css:193`, a `prefers-reduced-motion` block): there is **not one responsive breakpoint**, and this produces two *different* failures — at 390×844 `dashboard.html` contains its own overflow and silently crushes content (`grid3Cols: "23.3px 23.3px 23.3px"`, `tableW: 0`), while `exam-result.html?state=success` overflows to 544px with a 37px-wide CEFR track and a 56px score in a 101px box.

**Why it matters:** Three different real learners are excluded. Desktop-first is a recorded constraint; a `.grid-3` rendering 23px columns is not a trade-off, it is an unhandled case.
**Fix:** `aria-live="polite"` on `.save-status`, `role="timer"` on `.timer`. One visually-hidden `<h1>` per exam surface naming the current part. Two breakpoints (~1024px collapsing the shell to one column, ~640px collapsing the grids), plus `min-width` on `.cefr__track` and `.score-hero__total-value` so they degrade rather than implode.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt` then `/impeccable audit`

### [P1] The admin's core task is unverifiable and gives no feedback
**What:** The one thing the admin must do — compare the portrait against the CCCD photo — is asserted in copy (`kyc-review.html:181`) but visually unverifiable: all three `.kyc-photo` "images" are text placeholders. Then `:246-290` sends **both** "Từ chối" (with reason) and "Duyệt hồ sơ" to the identical `window.location.search = "?state=success"`. No row is removed, no confirmation names the record, and the sidebar `badge--danger` stays at "3". Combined with the missing close control, the dead Escape key, and focus escaping to the background (4 focusable in modal vs 9 behind), an admin processing a queue of rejections cannot tell which one they just actioned.

**Why it matters:** The highest-stakes human decision in the product is its least designed screen.
**Fix:** On approve/reject, remove the row, decrement the badge, and show a dismissible confirmation naming the learner. Add a `.modal__header` close control, wire Escape, trap focus while open. Replace at least one `.kyc-photo` placeholder with a real image so the comparison task can be evaluated.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden` then `/impeccable onboard`

## Questions to Consider

1. The state machine is the prototype's best asset and it is invisible to the learner — it exists for the reviewer via the switcher. What would it look like to surface "where am I, and what conditions do I still need to meet" as a persistent learner-facing element, so KYC, credits and readiness are visible as a checklist from registration rather than discovered one gate at a time?
2. If the diagnostic loop is the free differentiator, why does it appear only in the paid column? What is actually being sold — attempts, or the confidence that comes from knowing what to fix? If the latter, the pricing page is arguing against the product.
3. The KYC screen asks for a national ID and a selfie. The product has already made strong decisions about this (images not stored, CCCD hashed, masked number only) and none of them are in the interface. What is the minimum honest reassurance that gets a learner to hand those over — and does it belong on the KYC screen, or on the pricing page *before* they pay?
4. Every blocking screen offers "Luyện miễn phí". Is that a kindness, or an escape hatch that lets the product avoid explaining itself? What if the learner could see all three conditions for a real exam — verified, funded, ready — as a single state they are progressing through, rather than a wall they keep hitting?
5. The timer has three hardcoded values in markup and the target system makes the server authoritative. What should the client render in the 400ms before the first sync — nothing, a spinner, or a "syncing" state? The answer determines whether the learner ever sees a number they cannot trust.
6. What happens in the moment between submitting 200 questions and seeing a score? `exam-confirm-submit.html` models `only-submitting` for the *start* of an attempt — is there an equivalent, equally deliberate moment at the *end*, or does the learner jump straight from the last answer to a number? Under the peak-end rule that transition is the most memorable 30 seconds of the product, and it is currently unspecified.
